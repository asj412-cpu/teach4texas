Mid-October in a Texas Grade 3 room has a split personality. The benchmark window is close enough that you can feel it, and Friday still wants to feel like game day. So the stations come out, or a quiz-board file from a few years ago gets projected, and the hope is that the noise means the TEKS moved.

Grade 3 STAAR math does not take Friday off. Place value up to 100,000, two-step multiplication and division, fraction models, geometry and measurement, and data and money sense are all still on the clock. A loud block that only reviews bare facts leaves those standards sitting there for Monday.

The goal for the period is simple: keep the game-day energy, and make every cell count toward a TEKS.

## The TEKS clusters that belong on a Grade 3 review board

Name them the way you talk about them in planning, not as a standards dump. A useful Friday board has a few lanes, and these are the ones that actually show up in a Grade 3 review.

**Place value: compose, round, and compare (3.2A, 3.2C, 3.2D).** Students compose and decompose numbers up to 100,000, including expanded notation. They place a number between two consecutive multiples of 10, 100, 1,000, or 10,000 so rounding has a reason. They compare and order whole numbers up to 100,000 and show it with >, <, and =. In the room that sounds like: "Which of these is greater, and can you prove it with place value instead of the length of the numeral?" Some kids who can read 40,000 will still pick the number that merely looks longer.

**Operations, including the two-step (3.4A, 3.4D, 3.4K).** 3.4A is one-step and two-step addition and subtraction within 1,000, using place value, properties, and the relationship between the operations. 3.4D is the total when equal groups are combined or arranged in arrays up to 10 by 10. 3.4K is the one that is easiest to rush: one-step and two-step multiplication and division within 100, with objects, arrays, area models, equal groups, or facts students can recall. The trap is doing the first operation, seeing that number in the choices, and stopping. A two-step item is built to catch that.

**Fraction models (3.3A, 3.3B, 3.3C, 3.3F, 3.3H).** Denominators stay in the Grade 3 set: 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8. Students represent a fraction with objects, strip diagrams, and number lines; name the fraction for a point on a number line; explain that the unit fraction 1/b is one equal part of a whole split into b parts; show equivalent fractions with those denominators; and compare two fractions that share a numerator or a denominator. If the strip and the number line do not tell the same story, the choice is still wrong.

**Geometry, measurement, data, and money sense (3.6A, 3.6B, 3.6C, 3.7B, 3.7C, 3.8A, 3.8B, 3.9A, 3.9C).** This lane is easy to skip when stations only cover computation, and it is the rest of a real Grade 3 year. Students classify two- and three-dimensional figures with formal names, recognize rhombuses, parallelograms, trapezoids, rectangles and squares, and draw quadrilaterals that are none of these, and find the area of a rectangle as rows of unit squares. They find a perimeter or a missing side, and they add and subtract time intervals in minutes. On the data side they read a frequency table, dot plot, pictograph, or bar graph whose scale is not always 1, then solve a one- or two-step question from it. Personal financial literacy sits here too: the connection between work and income, and the costs and benefits of a planned spend versus an unplanned one.

## Where Friday game day usually goes sideways

**Quiz boards with bare facts.** A cell that says "6 × 7" and four products is retrieval practice. Grade 3 STAAR Math asks students to solve word problems and work with models, and a multiple-choice item can include the answer you get if you stop too early. Bare facts feel fast and teach the wrong habit.

**No TEKS labels.** If the category says "Numbers" and nothing else, you cannot tell whether the block practiced 3.2D or 3.4K. You also cannot tell a PLC what Friday covered. The standards have to be nameable when the board comes down.

**A PowerPoint that dies when kids yell.** Click-ahead slides freeze, the wrong team jumps the file, and you spend the period as the remote. The room is loud, the deck is lost, and the next class starts from a half-clicked board.

**A game that is only vibes.** Theme music and a buzzer do not make an item TEKS-true. If the questions are trivia, or the Daily Double is a joke, kids remember the noise and forget the model.

## What a useful live Grade 3 STAAR game show does

1. **The host projects one board.** A 5×5 board on the screen. You run the room. Students are not hunting through slides.
2. **Kids join on their own devices.** They enter a room code and play from their seats, so the noise is about the math.
3. **The items match Grade 3 STAAR Math.** Four choices on every cell. No calculator, the same as Grade 3 STAAR math. Word problems and models, in the style of the redesigned STAAR.
4. **The categories are the TEKS clusters above.** Place value, operations, fractions, geometry and measurement, and data and money sense, including two-step 3.4K.
5. **It fits one class period.** Twenty-five original items and two Daily Doubles, then you still have a minute to ask what stalled.
