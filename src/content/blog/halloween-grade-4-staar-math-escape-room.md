The real ask: keep the seasonal energy **while kids still practice STAAR Math** — multiply up to a four-digit by a one-digit and two-digit by two-digit, divide up to a four-digit dividend by a one-digit divisor, and represent multi-step problems with strip diagrams and equations — without turning October into a coloring book with a pumpkin sticker.

If your kids can finish a color-by-code and still freeze on a multi-step candy-sale story or a strip diagram on Thursday’s CFA, you already know the gap.

## The TEKS that matter in an October math block

Talk about them the way you plan, not as a standards dump.

**TEKS 4.4D** is multiplying up to a four-digit number by a one-digit number and two-digit by two-digit using strategies and the standard algorithm. In the room that means: can they finish a product under mild station pressure, not just circle a costume emoji next to a fact?

**TEKS 4.4E** is representing the quotient of a four-digit whole number dividend and a one-digit whole number divisor using arrays, area models, and equations — including the relationship to multiplication and remainders when they show up. Halloween week is a terrible time to park division. Fold it into the theme so it still feels like October, not a drill funeral.

**TEKS 4.5A** is representing multi-step problems involving the four operations with whole numbers using strip diagrams and equations with a letter standing for the unknown. That is the STAAR item shape teachers keep underestimating — kids who “get” the story still misread the strip or invent an equation that does not match the diagram.

Those three travel together in an escape format because missions can specialize, then a final lock mixes them the way a STAAR set does. One worksheet maze cannot carry that arc.

## Classroom pitfalls that look like “we did Halloween math”

**Color-by-number as the whole October plan.** Matching products to pumpkin colors keeps the room quiet. It does not practice 4.4D under mild pressure, 4.4E quotient representation, or 4.5A strip-diagram multi-step work. Save the coloring for the last five minutes — do not let it eat the block.

**Seasonal sticker, wrong TEKS.** A deck labeled “Halloween Grade 4” that only hits 3rd-grade facts or never asks for a strip diagram is busywork wearing a costume. Name the TEKS on the student page.

**Escape rooms that are really worksheets with a lock icon.** If kids never earn a code from correct math — or never talk through why the equation matches the strip — you ran a packet, not a mission.

**No self-checking path.** Halloween week is chaotic. You need Google Slides that loop wrong answers with a hint, plus a printable tracker you can scan from the door. A teacher-at-every-desk debrief burns the planning period you do not have.
