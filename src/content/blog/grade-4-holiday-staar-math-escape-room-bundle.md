October, November, and December in a Texas Grade 4 room are three short, loud stretches. Halloween week, the days before Thanksgiving, and the last full week before winter break. Assemblies and parties eat the schedule, and the easy move each time is a coloring page with a seasonal border.

Grade 4 STAAR math is still on the calendar. The plan that survives those weeks is already decided before the week gets chopped up: one seasonal escape room per month. October carries multiplication, division, and strip diagrams. November carries equal-denominator fractions, remainders, and measurement. December carries comparing fractions, rectangle perimeter and area, and data displays. That is nine TEKS across three class periods. Each month hits a different set.

## October: Halloween, TEKS 4.4D, 4.4E, 4.5A

[The Halloween guide](/blog/halloween-grade-4-staar-math-escape-room) has the longer classroom write-up. In planning language:

**TEKS 4.4D** is multiplying up to a four-digit number by a one-digit number and two-digit by two-digit, with strategies and the standard algorithm.

**TEKS 4.4E** is representing the quotient of a four-digit dividend and a one-digit divisor, using arrays, area models, and equations, including the tie to multiplication and remainders when they show up.

**TEKS 4.5A** is representing multi-step problems with the four operations and whole numbers, using strip diagrams and equations with a letter for the unknown.

The room is self-checking Cadet Corps Google Slides plus printable missions, about 45 to 60 minutes. Run the missions in that one block and collect the trackers on the way out. The guide names two misses for the next morning: a 4.5A strip that does not match the equation, and a 4.4E remainder the class skipped.

## November: Thanksgiving, TEKS 4.3E, 4.4H, 4.8C

[The Thanksgiving guide](/blog/thanksgiving-grade-4-staar-math-escape-room) walks the feast story. In the room:

**TEKS 4.3E** is adding and subtracting fractions with equal denominators, with models that build toward the number line. The miss to watch is adding the denominators too, so 5/8 + 2/8 comes back as 7/16.

**TEKS 4.4H** is dividing up to a four-digit dividend by a one-digit divisor and interpreting the remainder. Thirty guests at tables of 4 need 8 tables, because the two leftover guests still need a seat.

**TEKS 4.8C** is solving measurement problems — length, intervals of time, liquid volume, mass, and money — with the operation that fits. The Thanksgiving guide notes that 4.8C is a readiness standard, and many of the items take two steps.

Run it in one class period, about 45 to 60 minutes, in pairs, with no calculator. The path is Pie Kitchen (4.3E), Dinner Table (4.4H), Harvest Market (4.8C), Leftover Lab (two-step 4.8C), then the Gratitude Gate, which mixes all three. That is 20 STAAR 2.0-style items, self-checking Google Slides, printable missions, a recording sheet, a teacher guide, and an answer key. Pull the recording sheets for the remainder that should have rounded up, and the measurement item where the class stopped after the first step.

The Thanksgiving room also includes a live game bonus on [play.teach4texas.com](https://play.teach4texas.com/). The access code is in its Start Here PDF, and that PDF is in the holiday bundle. The class-period plan above is the escape room.

## December: Christmas, TEKS 4.3D, 4.5D, 4.9A

[The Christmas guide](/blog/christmas-grade-4-staar-math-escape-room) covers compare-fractions, area, and data. TEA's Grade 4 mathematics assessed curriculum labels these three readiness standards ([TEA, revised August 2026](https://tea.texas.gov/data-reports/staar/sst-4-math-assessed-curriculum.pdf)). The same document says these content standards stay the same for the Student Success Tool, which TEA plans to start in fall 2027.

**TEKS 4.3D** is comparing two fractions with different numerators and different denominators and recording the comparison with >, =, or <. Put 5/8 and 3/4 on the board before anyone opens a mission. The bigger numerator and the bigger denominator both point at 5/8. 3/4 is 6/8, so 3/4 is greater. A number line, or a drawn model of the same whole, makes that visible.

**TEKS 4.5D** is perimeter and area of rectangles with whole-number sides. Have kids say the unit out loud: perimeter in inches, area in square inches. A sheet of wrapping paper that is 8 inches by 5 inches has a perimeter of 26 inches and an area of 40 square inches. If the perimeter is 28 inches and the length is 9 inches, the width is 5 inches, because half of 28 is 14 and 14 − 9 = 5. The area of that 9-by-5 rectangle is 45 square inches. Students who stop after the width choose 5 and never finish the area.

**TEKS 4.9A** is representing data on a frequency table, a dot plot, or a stem-and-leaf plot marked with whole numbers and fractions. A December rep should include a stem-and-leaf plot and at least one display marked with a fraction. A page of whole-number tallies leaves part of the standard off the table.

The room is about 45 to 60 minutes, in pairs, with no calculator. Cookie Kitchen is 4.3D, Wrapping Room is 4.5D, Toy Count Depot is 4.9A, Sleigh Hangar is two-step 4.5D, and the Gift Gate mixes the three. Twenty STAAR 2.0-style items, and the rectangle problems include missing side lengths. Rectangle, number-line, and frequency-table items include a drawn model. Assign it the way the Start Here sheet says: open the force-copy link so the deck lands in your Drive, then in Google Classroom choose "Make a copy for each student." Students play in Present mode. If the week only gives you two short blocks, run Cookie Kitchen and Wrapping Room the first day, then Toy Count Depot, Sleigh Hangar, and Gift Gate the next.

## Where a three-month plan usually goes sideways

**A coloring page as the math block.** The room gets quiet. That month's TEKS never gets a rep.

**One standard in three borders.** October, November, and December above are nine different TEKS. A multiply page with a new clip-art stamp each month is still 4.4D.

**All three rooms in the last week before winter break.** October's multiplication needs a normal week. Put one room on each month.

**A five-day unit in a two-day week.** You need one class period, or the two-day split December sometimes forces. Scenes that specialize, then a mixed finish, fit that week better than a new mini-unit.

STAAR does not permit a calculator on the grades 3–7 mathematics assessments unless a student is eligible for one as a designated support ([TEA STAAR Calculator Policy](https://tea2.tea.texas.gov/data-reports/staar/staar-calculator-policy.pdf)). November and December are written for pairs. Each room leaves a tracker or a recording sheet you can look at the next morning.
