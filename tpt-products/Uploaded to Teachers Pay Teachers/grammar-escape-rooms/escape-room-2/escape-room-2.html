🔍 Detective Grammar — Teacher Guide

Escape Room 2 | 4th Grade | 30–45 Minutes

📋 Overview

Students solve a mystery by visiting 5 crime scenes and fixing grammar errors to collect evidence letters. Unscramble the letters to reveal the thief’s name.

TEKS: 4.11D.vi (pronouns), 4.11D.ix (punctuation including commas)

Skills: Subject-verb agreement, pronoun-antecedent agreement, comma usage

🖥️ How to Assign in Google Classroom

  1. Open Google Classroom → Classwork → + Create → Assignment
  2. Attach the Google Slides file → “Make a copy for each student”
  3. Instructions: “Work through each crime scene in order. Record your evidence letters on the Evidence Tracker (Slide 3). Unscramble the letters at the end to catch the thief!”
  4. Assign!

⏰ Timing

Setup Activity Wrap-Up
5 min 25–35 min 5–10 min

Wrap-Up Suggestion: Review Scene 5 (mixed review) together — it’s the hardest and generates the best discussion.

🎯 Differentiation

Struggling Learners:

  • Provide a grammar rules reference sheet (SVA, pronouns, commas)
  • Allow partner work
  • Focus on Scenes 1, 3, and 4 (skip Scene 2 which has tricky SVA)

Advanced Learners:

  • Challenge: Find additional errors the detective “missed” (write their own)
  • Time challenge: Complete in under 25 minutes
  • Write a “Case Report” paragraph using correct SVA, pronouns, and commas

ELL Students:

  • Pre-teach: subject, verb, pronoun, antecedent, comma
  • Allow native language grammar reference
  • Pair with bilingual buddy

🏫 Center vs. Whole Class

Center:

  • 1–2 rotation periods
  • Self-directed, minimal teacher support needed
  • Print Evidence Tracker for paper option

Whole Class:

  • Project and discuss after each scene
  • Great for grammar review before assessments
  • Use “think-pair-share” for each challenge

🎮 Extension Activities

  1. Write the News Article: Students write a Grammar City Gazette article about the thief being caught — must use correct SVA, pronouns, and commas
  2. Create a New Crime Scene: Students write their own Scene 6 with 4 grammar questions
  3. Pronoun Swap: Give students a paragraph and have them replace nouns with correct pronouns
  4. Comma Surgery: Give students unpunctuated paragraphs to add commas

📊 Assessment

  • Formative assessment for subject-verb agreement, pronoun usage, and comma rules
  • Great pre-test/post-test activity for grammar unit
  • Works as STAAR grammar prep (4th grade conventions)

🔍 Detective Grammar — Answer Key

Escape Room 2 | 4th Grade

🔴 Scene 1: The Library — Subject-Verb Agreement (Letter: R)

# Sentence Correct Explanation
1 The group of students ___ ready. is “Group” is the subject (singular), not “students.”
2 My brothers ___ soccer every Saturday. play “Brothers” is plural → plural verb “play.”
3 The cat and the dog ___ in the yard. run Compound subject (cat AND dog) = plural.
4 Everyone in the class ___ a pencil. needs “Everyone” is always singular.

Common Mistakes: Students pick the verb that matches the nearest noun instead of the true subject. “Group of students” — students is NOT the subject; it’s part of a prepositional phrase.

🟡 Scene 2: The Park — Tricky SVA (Letter: A)

# Correct Sentence Explanation
1 The box of chocolates was on the table. “Box” is singular. “Of chocolates” is a prepositional phrase.
2 Neither the teacher nor the students were late. With “neither…nor,” verb matches the CLOSER subject (“students” = plural).
3 Here comes the marching band! Inverted sentence — “band” is singular.
4 The news is on at six o’clock. “News” is a singular noun despite the -s ending.

Common Mistakes: “Neither…nor” rule is the trickiest. Also, students think “news” is plural.

🟢 Scene 3: The Museum — Pronoun-Antecedent (Letter: V)

# Sentence Correct Explanation
1 Each student…own supplies. his or her “Each” is singular → singular pronoun.
2 The players celebrated…victory. their “Players” is plural → “their.”
3 Maria and José finished…project. their Two people = plural → “their.”
4 The dog wagged…tail. its Animal/thing → “its” (no apostrophe!).
5 Somebody left…backpack. his or her “Somebody” is singular indefinite pronoun.

Common Mistakes: Using “their” for singular indefinite pronouns (each, somebody, everyone). While common in speech, standard grammar uses “his or her.” Also: “it’s” vs. “its” confusion.

🔵 Scene 4: The Restaurant — Commas (Letter: E)

# Correct Rule Applied
1 milk, eggs, and bread Commas in a series/list
2 After the storm, the rainbow… Comma after introductory phrase
3 …play outside, but it was raining. Comma before conjunction in compound sentence
4 Yes, I would like some more, please. Comma after introductory word + before “please”
5 blue, green, and purple Commas in a series/list

Common Mistakes: Forgetting the comma before the conjunction in compound sentences. Also, students often omit the Oxford comma (the one before “and” in a list) — either convention is acceptable, but consistency matters.

🟣 Scene 5: The School — Mixed Review (Letter: N)

# Sentence Error Correction Explanation
1 The team are playing… SVA “are” → “is” “Team” is singular collective noun.
2 …me and my friend walks… SVA + pronoun “My friend and I walk” “I” not “me” as subject; compound subject = plural verb.
3 Sarah brought her lunch but she… Comma Add comma: “lunch, but” Compound sentence needs comma before “but.”
4 Each of the boys need…their… SVA + pronoun “needs…his” “Each” is singular → singular verb and pronoun.

Common Mistakes: Q2 has TWO errors, which is the hardest. Students often catch one but miss the other.
