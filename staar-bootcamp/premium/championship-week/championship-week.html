🏈 STAAR Athletes: Championship Week — Quick-Start Overview

Welcome, Coach!

You’ve just downloaded Championship Week — a sports-themed STAAR prep week that frames the entire test as the Big Game. Your students are athletes in training, you’re the head coach, and STAAR day is game day. This week is their pre-season training camp.

📦 What’s in This Package

🏆 The Theme at a Glance

  • Setting: Championship tournament / Training facility
  • Students are: Athletes preparing for the big game
  • Teacher is: Head Coach
  • Each day is: A round in the tournament (Quarterfinals → Semifinals → etc.)
  • Completing work earns: MVP Awards & advancement on the bracket
  • The week builds to: Game Day (STAAR test day)
  • Hallway becomes: Stadium tunnel entrance
  • Classroom becomes: Team locker room / training facility

📅 Week Overview

Day Round Focus
Monday Quarterfinals: Draft Day Foundation skills, team building, setting goals
Tuesday Semifinals: Practice Drills Focused skill review — math/ELA deep work
Wednesday Halftime Show Fun review games, midweek energy boost
Thursday Championship Round: Scrimmage Full STAAR-format practice under game conditions

⏰ How to Run This Week

2 Weeks Before

Weekend Before

Each Morning

  1. Play pump-up sports music as students enter
  2. Deliver the Coach’s Huddle (daily kickoff speech)
  3. Hand out daily playbook pages
  4. Run the day’s drills and activities
  5. Award Daily MVP at end of day
  6. Update tournament bracket — class advances!

💡 Pro Tips

  1. Wear a whistle. Blow it to start activities. Kids lose their minds (in a good way).
  2. Use sports language constantly. “Let’s huddle up!” “That’s a championship answer!” “We need to run that drill again.”
  3. The bracket is everything. Students LOVE seeing themselves advance through a tournament.
  4. Friday pep rally should be loud. Play music, do a class cheer, make it feel like a real pre-game.
  5. Team names matter. Let table groups pick their team names on Monday. They’ll take ownership.

🏈 STAAR Athletes: Championship Week — 5-Day Lesson Plans

Head Coach’s Playbook — Your game plan for the week. Adapt to your grade level (3rd–8th) and tested subjects.

🏈 MONDAY — Quarterfinals: Draft Day

Theme: “Every championship team starts with a draft. Today we assess our roster and build our teams.”

Focus: Foundation skills assessment & team building

Coach’s Huddle (10 min)

Teacher Script: > “Athletes! Welcome to Championship Week! I’m Coach [Name], and this week we’re training for the biggest game of the year — STAAR. Every champion trains before game day. Michael Jordan didn’t just show up and win — he practiced harder than anyone. This week, that’s us. Today is Draft Day. We’re building our teams, assessing our skills, and getting ready to compete. Let’s go, athletes!”

Activities: - Hand out Player Jerseys (name tags — see STUDENT-MATERIALS.md) - Assign team names (let groups choose from options or make their own) - Reveal the Tournament Bracket poster - Quick “skills combine” — 5-question diagnostic to see where students are

Practice Drill 1: Guided Review (45 min)

ELA/Reading Focus: 1. (10 min) “Warm-Up Laps” — 5 quick review questions on whiteboards. “Just like stretching before a game — gotta warm up those brains!” 2. (20 min) “Film Study” — Teacher models reading a STAAR passage like studying game film. “Watch how I break down this play…” Demonstrate annotation, evidence-finding, question analysis. 3. (15 min) “Partner Drills” — Pairs work through a short passage together. One reads, one annotates. Discuss answers. “A great teammate makes everyone better.”

Water Break (5 min)

  • “Stretches & Sprints” — students stand, do 30 seconds of exercises (jumping jacks, arm circles, running in place), then sit. Quick physical reset.

Practice Drill 2: Independent Practice (40 min)

“Quarterfinal Playbook” Packet - STAAR-format questions covering today’s focus area - Frame: “This is your first game. Show Coach what you’ve got.” - Early finishers: “Bonus Play” challenge sheet

Differentiation: - Struggling: “Rookie Edition” — shorter passages, vocabulary pre-taught, guided questions - Advanced: “All-Star Challenge” — complex texts, analytical questions - ELL: Bilingual glossary, simplified question stems

Post-Game Wrap (10 min)

  • Review key plays (most-missed questions)
  • Coach’s Chalkboard: Write the day’s key strategy (like diagramming a play)
  • Award Monday MVP — 1-2 students who showed exceptional effort
  • Update bracket: “We won the quarterfinals! Moving on to semifinals!”

🏈 TUESDAY — Semifinals: Practice Drills

Theme: “Semifinals are when it gets real. Today we drill the fundamentals until they’re automatic.”

Focus: Mathematics (or your primary math-tested area)

Coach’s Huddle (10 min)

Teacher Script: > “Athletes, yesterday we won the quarterfinals. Today’s the semis — this is where pretenders get eliminated and real athletes shine. What separates good from great? PRACTICE. The fundamentals. Today we drill math until these skills are muscle memory. When you see a problem on STAAR, your brain should react automatically — like a point guard running a fast break. Let’s drill!”

Practice Drill 1 (45 min)

Math Focus (adapt to grade): 1. (10 min) “Sprint Drill” — Timed computation problems (2 min each set). “Quick feet, quick calculations!” 2. (20 min) “Play Diagramming” — Teacher models word problems step by step, drawing them out like diagramming a play on the chalkboard. Show work visually. “Here’s the play. Step 1: read the defense (the question). Step 2: identify your options. Step 3: execute.” 3. (15 min) “Two-a-Days” — Partners take turns being “coach” and “player.” Coach reads a problem, player solves while thinking aloud. Coach checks. Switch.

Water Break (5 min)

  • “Timeout!” — Quick trivia game (non-academic sports trivia). Fun mental break.

Practice Drill 2 (40 min)

“Semifinal Playbook” Packet - 15-20 STAAR-format math problems - Include mix: computation, word problems, griddable, multi-step - “This is the semifinal. Leave it all on the field.”

Differentiation: - Struggling: Formula reference sheet included, worked examples at top, fewer problems - Advanced: “Varsity-level” problems — multi-step, real-world application - ELL: Simplified word problem language, key math vocabulary visuals

Post-Game Wrap (10 min)

  • Review 2-3 key problems
  • Coach’s Chalkboard: Day’s strategy on the board
  • Award Tuesday MVP
  • Bracket update: “Semifinals — DONE. Championship round is Thursday.”

🏈 WEDNESDAY — Halftime Show 🎉

Theme: “Every great game has a halftime show. Time to catch our breath, have some fun, and refocus for the second half.”

Focus: Fun review games, mixed subjects, energy boost

Coach’s Huddle (10 min)

Teacher Script: > “We’re at the halfway point, athletes. You’ve been grinding hard — two days of intense training done. Today is our Halftime Show. That doesn’t mean we stop working — it means we train differently. Today is about GAMES, teamwork, and having fun while reviewing. Sometimes the best practice is play. Let’s enjoy this!”

Practice Drill 1: Halftime Games (45 min)

Run 2-3 of the following (see ACTIVITIES.md for full instructions):

  1. (15 min) “STAAR Bowl” — Jeopardy-style review game. Categories by subject/topic. Teams compete, keep score on the class “scoreboard.”

  2. (15 min) “Relay Race” — Teams line up, relay-style. Each member runs to solve one problem. First team to finish all problems correctly wins.

  3. (15 min) “Trading Card Swap” — Students create test-strategy trading cards, then trade with classmates to collect a full set (see ACTIVITIES.md).

Water Break (5 min)

  • Play pump-up music. Let students talk/socialize briefly. Real halftime vibes.

Practice Drill 2: Mixed Review (40 min)

“Halftime Report” Mixed Practice - Shorter packet than other days — 10-12 mixed-subject questions - Frame: “Quick second-half tune-up. Stay sharp.” - Remaining time: Study Hall — students review their weak areas using flashcards, playbooks, or past packets

Post-Game Wrap (10 min)

  • Motivational Moment: Share a story of a sports comeback (28-3 Super Bowl, Astros 2017 WS, etc. — Texas teams get bonus points!)
  • Award Wednesday MVP
  • Bracket: “Halftime done. Second half starts tomorrow. Championship round!”
  • Hand out midweek treats if desired

🏈 THURSDAY — Championship Round: Scrimmage

Theme: “This is it. The championship scrimmage. Game-like conditions. Everything you’ve trained for.”

Focus: Full STAAR-format practice test (simulated conditions)

Coach’s Huddle (10 min)

Teacher Script: > “Athletes. Today is the championship scrimmage. A scrimmage is a practice game under real conditions. I need you to treat this like the real thing. Game faces on. No talking. No distractions. Show me that you can perform when it matters. I’ve seen you train all week — I KNOW you can do this. Now prove it to yourselves.”

Practice Drill 1: Scrimmage (45 min)

Championship Scrimmage Test - Mini STAAR-format test (20-30 mixed questions) - Test conditions: silent, independent, timed - “This is the championship. Play like a champion.”

Water Break (5 min)

  • Quiet stretch. Maintain intensity.

Practice Drill 2: Film Review (40 min)

Post-Scrimmage Analysis: 1. (15 min) Review answers as a class — “Let’s watch the game film and see what we can improve.” 2. (10 min) Students self-identify weak areas on an assessment sheet 3. (15 min) Small group “position coaching” — group students by need. Teacher and peer tutors provide targeted review.

Post-Game Wrap (10 min)

  • Coach’s Chalkboard: Final strategies
  • Award Thursday MVP
  • Bracket: “Championship round COMPLETE. Tomorrow: PRE-GAME PREP.”
  • Serious moment: “Tomorrow we celebrate. But right now, I want you to know — I’m proud of every single one of you.”

🏈 FRIDAY — Pre-Game Prep: Pep Rally 🎉

Theme: “Game day is almost here. Today we sharpen our strategies, build our confidence, and celebrate how far we’ve come.”

Focus: Test-taking strategies, confidence building, celebration

Coach’s Huddle (10 min)

Teacher Script: > “Athletes! This is it — the last day of training camp. You have worked SO hard. You ran drills. You studied film. You survived the championship scrimmage. Today is PRE-GAME PREP. We’re going to review our game plan, get our minds right, and finish with a PEP RALLY. Because when you walk into that test, you’re walking in as CHAMPIONS.”

Practice Drill 1: Strategy Session (45 min)

  1. (15 min) “Coach’s Top 10 Game-Day Plays” — Review the 10 most important test-taking strategies (see STAAR Wars LESSON-PLANS.md Friday for the list — same strategies, sports language)

  2. (15 min) “Scouting Report” — Analyze common STAAR “trick plays” (misleading answer choices). Students practice identifying traps.

  3. (15 min) “Captain’s Speech” — Students write/share one thing they’ve learned and one piece of advice for their teammates. 2-3 volunteers share with the class.

Water Break (5 min)

Practice Drill 2: Pep Rally & Celebration (40 min)

  1. (10 min) “Letters to Myself” — Students write a game-day letter, sealed in envelope for test day
  2. (10 min) “MVP Certificate Ceremony” — Award Championship Certificates to every student. Call each name, handshake, photo.
  3. (10 min) “Team Cheer” — Class creates a short STAAR cheer together. Practice it. It’s silly. It’s fun. They’ll remember it on test day.
  4. (10 min) Celebration — Pump-up music, treats, students sign each other’s “Championship Program” page

Post-Game Wrap — Final Whistle (10 min)

  • All 5 MVPs earned = Champion status
  • Final cheer together
  • “When you sit down for that STAAR test, remember: you TRAINED for this. Champions don’t hope to win — they PREPARE to win. And you’ve prepared. Now go win.”

🏈 STAAR Athletes: Championship Week — Themed Activities

12 sports-themed activities for your Championship Week. Mix of drills, games, and team challenges.

🎮 REVIEW GAMES

1. STAAR Bowl (Jeopardy-Style Game)

Type: Teams | Time: 25-30 min | Best for: Multi-subject review

Setup: - Create a 5x5 grid on the board (5 categories, 5 point values: 100-500) - Categories: Reading Plays, Math Offense, Science Defense, Vocabulary Blitz, Wild Card - Divide class into 3-4 teams

How to Play: 1. Teams take turns selecting a category and point value 2. Read the question; team has 30 seconds to discuss and answer 3. Correct = earn points. Incorrect = other teams can steal 4. “Daily Double” — one hidden square doubles the points 5. Team with most points wins the STAAR Bowl

Materials: Grid on board, question cards, scoreboard

2. Relay Race Review

Type: Teams of 5-6 | Time: 15-20 min | Best for: Quick computation, grammar, vocabulary

Setup: - Teams line up single-file facing the board - Each team has a set of questions at the front (face down)

How to Play: 1. First player runs up, flips a question, solves it, writes answer 2. Runs back, tags next teammate 3. Next player runs up, checks previous answer (fix if wrong), then solves the next question 4. First team to correctly answer all questions wins 5. Penalty: wrong answer = team must do 5 jumping jacks before continuing

Materials: Question sets (one per team), pencils, answer key

3. March Madness Bracket Challenge

Type: Individual/class | Time: Ongoing all week | Best for: Daily engagement

Setup: - Create a tournament bracket on poster board with 16 “skills” (e.g., Main Idea, Fractions, Vocabulary, etc.) - Each day, two skills “compete” — students answer questions from both skills - The skill the class scores higher on “advances”

How to Play: 1. Monday: 4 matchups (8 skills compete, 4 advance) 2. Tuesday: 2 matchups (4 skills, 2 advance) 3. Wednesday: Halftime — Fun bracket (favorite snacks, songs, etc.) 4. Thursday: Championship — final 2 skills compete 5. Friday: Crown the class’s “Strongest Skill”

Materials: Large bracket poster, daily question sets for each matchup

👤 INDIVIDUAL ACTIVITIES

4. Personal Playbook (Study Guide Creation)

Type: Individual | Time: 20-25 min | Best for: Any subject synthesis

Instructions: Students create their own “playbook” — a folded booklet with their personal study notes organized as “plays”: - Offensive Plays = Strategies for questions they’re good at (how to maintain those skills) - Defensive Plays = Strategies for their weak areas (how to handle tough questions) - Special Teams = Test-taking tricks (elimination, time management, etc.) - Audibles = What to do when stuck (skip and come back, re-read, etc.)

Materials: Paper (folded into booklet), pencils, colored markers

5. Strategy Trading Cards

Type: Individual (then partner swap) | Time: 20 min | Best for: Reviewing test-taking strategies

Instructions: Each student creates 3 trading cards (index cards): - Front: Strategy name + illustration (e.g., “The Eliminator” with a picture of crossing out wrong answers) - Back: How to use the strategy, when it works best, example - After creation, students TRADE cards with classmates to collect different strategies - Goal: collect at least 8 different strategy cards by end of week

Strategy ideas: The Eliminator, The Re-Reader, The Evidence Hunter, The Show-Your-Worker, The Time Manager, The Double Checker, The Process of Elimination Pro, The Context Clue Detective, The Key Word Finder, The Reasonableness Checker

Materials: Index cards, markers, colored pencils

6. MVP Scouting Report (Self-Assessment)

Type: Individual | Time: 15 min | Best for: Metacognition, goal setting

Instructions: Students fill out a “scouting report” on themselves: - Player Name: [their name] - Position: [their strongest subject] - Strengths (Offensive Stats): List 3 skills they’re confident in - Areas to Develop (Defensive Stats): List 2-3 skills they need to work on - Game Plan: For each weak area, write one specific action they’ll take - Season Goal: “On STAAR day, I will score _____ because I will _____”

Materials: Scouting Report template (printed), pencils

👥 PARTNER ACTIVITIES

7. Coach & Athlete Pairs

Type: Partner | Time: 20-25 min | Best for: Math problem solving, reading comprehension

Instructions: - Partner A = Coach (guides, questions, doesn’t solve) - Partner B = Athlete (solves, thinks aloud) - Coach uses prompts: “What’s the play here?”, “Show me your work,” “Are you sure about that call?” - Switch roles every 2-3 problems - Both partners record answers and strategies

Materials: Problem set (8-10 problems), recording sheet

8. Two-Minute Drill (Speed Partner Quiz)

Type: Partner | Time: 10-15 min | Best for: Fact fluency, vocabulary, quick recall

Instructions: - Partners face each other with a stack of flashcards or question strips between them - Set timer for 2 minutes - Partner A quizzes Partner B as fast as possible — keep a tally of correct answers - Switch roles for another 2 minutes - Compare scores — both try to beat their own score in round 2 - “In the two-minute drill, every second counts!”

Materials: Flashcards or question strips, timer, tally sheet

👥👥 GROUP ACTIVITIES

9. Scrimmage Stations (Rotation Drill)

Type: Groups of 4-5 | Time: 30-35 min | Best for: Multi-subject review

Setup: 5 stations, each themed as a different “position”: - Quarterback Station — Reading comprehension (leads the offense = understands the text) - Running Back Station — Math computation (runs the numbers) - Wide Receiver Station — Vocabulary (catches the meaning) - Defensive Line Station — Error analysis (find the mistakes) - Kicker Station — Word problems (precision under pressure)

How to Play: Groups rotate every 6 minutes. Complete 3-4 questions per station. Record on scorecard.

Materials: Station question sets, scorecards, timer, station labels

10. Huddle Up: Team Problem Solving

Type: Groups of 4 | Time: 20 min | Best for: Complex multi-step problems

Instructions: 1. Each group gets ONE complex, multi-step problem (challenging!) 2. Team huddles — discuss strategy before anyone writes anything (2 min) 3. Team works together to solve (8 min) 4. One team member presents their solution to the class (2 min each) 5. Class votes: “Did they make the right call?” — discuss correct approach

Materials: One complex problem per group (laminated or on large paper), markers, chart paper for presenting

11. Championship Bracket Debate

Type: Groups of 4 (2v2) | Time: 20 min | Best for: Reading/ELA, Social Studies

Instructions: - Present a STAAR question with 2 plausible answer choices - In each group, 2 students argue for Answer A, 2 for Answer B - Each side presents evidence (2 min per side) - Group votes, then teacher reveals correct answer - Winning side earns a point for their team on the class scoreboard

Materials: Debate questions with passages, timer, scoreboard

12. Tailgate Party Review (End-of-Day Celebration Game)

Type: Whole class | Time: 20 min | Best for: End of Wednesday (Halftime Show)

Setup: - “Tailgate” stations around the room with snacks and games - Station 1: Vocabulary Cornhole (toss beanbags at vocab words, define the one you hit) - Station 2: Math Ring Toss (toss rings onto numbered pegs, solve the problem on that number) - Station 3: Trivia Toss (answer question correctly, earn a free throw with a paper ball into a trash can) - Station 4: Word Problem Horseshoes (solve problem, toss horseshoe — closest to the stake gets a bonus point)

Keep it simple — use what you have. Paper balls and trash cans work great. The point is FUN with learning mixed in.

Materials: Paper balls, trash cans, beanbags (or balled-up socks), vocab cards, math problems, small snacks

🏟️ HALLWAY TRANSFORMATION: Stadium Tunnel

The Vision

Students walk through a “tunnel” into the stadium — just like athletes running onto the field. School colors, team banners, and a scoreboard create game-day energy.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1. Stadium Tunnel Entrance

  • Hang streamers (school colors) from the ceiling/doorframe at the hallway entrance, creating a curtain students push through
  • Alternative: cut black butcher paper into strips and hang like a curtain
  • Add a banner above: “CHAMPIONSHIP WEEK — STAAR ATHLETES ENTER HERE”
  • If allowed, play entrance music from a speaker as students walk through on Monday morning
  • Cost: ~$3-5 for streamers

2. Locker Room Door Signs

  • Each classroom door gets a “locker” treatment:
    • Teacher name as “Head Coach [Name]”
    • Subject as position: “Offensive Coordinator — Mathematics”
    • Team logo (school mascot or custom design)
  • Print on colored cardstock (school colors), laminate
  • Optional: tape a jersey number above each door (large printed number)
  • Cost: ~$2-3 for cardstock

3. Hallway Scoreboard

  • Create a large scoreboard poster in the hallway
  • Title: “CHAMPIONSHIP SCOREBOARD”
  • List each class period / homeroom as a “team”
  • Track daily completion rates, participation, or scores
  • Update daily — creates friendly competition between classes
  • Cost: ~$2 for poster board
