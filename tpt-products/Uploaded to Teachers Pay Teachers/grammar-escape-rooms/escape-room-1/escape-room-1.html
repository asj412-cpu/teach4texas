🚀 The Grammar Galaxy — Teacher Guide

Escape Room 1 | 3rd Grade | 30–45 Minutes

📋 Overview

Students navigate through 5 planets in the Grammar Galaxy, solving parts-of-speech and sentence structure challenges to collect code digits and escape. This is a self-paced, interactive Google Slides activity.

TEKS Alignment: 3.11D.vii (editing for parts of speech), 3.11D.i (complete sentences)

Skills Covered: Nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, complete sentences vs. fragments

🖥️ How to Assign in Google Classroom

  1. Open Google Classroom → Click the class → Classwork tab
  2. Click + Create → Assignment
  3. Click Add → Google Drive → Select the Grammar Galaxy Slides file
  4. ⚠️ IMPORTANT: Under the file, change the dropdown to “Make a copy for each student”
  5. Add instructions: “Work through each planet in order. Record your code digits on the Clue Tracker (Slide 3). Enter the Star Code at the end to escape!”
  6. Set point value (suggested: 100 points or Completion grade)
  7. Set due date and Assign

Tip: Preview the slides in Present mode first so you can demo the clicking/navigation for students.

⏰ Timing

Setup Activity Wrap-Up
5 min 25–35 min 5 min
  • Setup: Explain the concept, demo Slide 1–3, show how to click answers
  • Activity: Students work independently or in pairs
  • Wrap-Up: Celebrate who escaped, review tricky questions

🎯 Tips for Differentiation

For Struggling Learners:

  • Pair with a stronger student (partner escape)
  • Provide an anchor chart of parts of speech definitions
  • Let students use the hint slides without penalty
  • Pre-teach vocabulary in the sentences before starting

For Advanced Learners:

  • Challenge them to complete it in under 20 minutes
  • Have them write their OWN sentence for each planet after completing
  • Ask them to explain WHY each wrong answer is wrong
  • Extension: Create a 6th planet with their own questions

For ELL Students:

  • Pre-teach key vocabulary (eagle, ancient, bloom, peacefully)
  • Allow use of bilingual dictionary
  • Pair with a bilingual buddy

🏫 Center vs. Whole Class

As a Literacy Center (Recommended):

  • Load on classroom devices/Chromebooks
  • Students rotate through during center time
  • Takes 1–2 center rotations to complete
  • Print the Clue Tracker slide for students who prefer paper tracking

As a Whole Class Activity:

  • Project and work through together (less ideal — removes self-pacing)
  • Better option: Everyone starts together, works at own pace
  • Use a visible timer for motivation
  • First 5 to escape get a small prize/recognition

🎮 Extension Activities

  1. Create Your Own Planet: Students design a 6th planet with 4 questions on a part of speech of their choice
  2. Grammar Galaxy Journal: Students write a short story using at least 3 nouns, 3 verbs, 3 adjectives, and 3 adverbs — underline and label each
  3. Parts of Speech Sort: Give students a passage and have them sort words into noun/verb/adjective/adverb columns
  4. Fragment Fix-It: Students take the fragments from Room 5 and rewrite them as complete sentences

🔧 Troubleshooting

Issue Solution
Links don’t work Make sure students are in Present mode (not Edit)
Student is stuck Direct them to re-read the hint slide; check if they’re clicking the answer shapes
Student finishes early Extension activities above, or have them help a classmate
Wrong code at the end Have student revisit the room where they’re missing a digit

📊 Assessment

This activity works as: - ✅ Formative assessment (observe which rooms students struggle with) - ✅ Practice/review activity - ✅ Test prep (parts of speech identification) - ✅ Sub plans (self-directed, minimal teacher involvement needed) - ✅ Completion grade or participation points

To grade for accuracy: Use the Answer Key to check student responses on their individual copy of the slides.

🔴 Room 1: Planet Noun (Digit: 7)

# Sentence Correct Answer Why It’s Correct
1 The ___ flew across the sky. B) eagle Eagle is a thing (animal) — a noun. “Quickly” is an adverb; “flew” is a verb.
2 We visited the tall ___ downtown. A) building Building is a thing/place — a noun. “Visited” is a verb; “tall” is an adjective.
3 Her ___ made everyone smile. C) kindness Kindness is an idea — an abstract noun. “Made” is a verb; “everyone” is a pronoun (accept if students choose this, but kindness is the target noun in the blank).
4 The ___ played in the park. B) children Children is a person (people) — a noun. “Played” is a verb; “in” is a preposition.

Common Mistakes: - Confusing verbs ending in -ing with nouns (e.g., “running” can be both — teach context) - Missing abstract nouns like “kindness” — students often only think of concrete nouns - Choosing “everyone” in Q3 — it IS a pronoun/noun, but the blank targets “kindness”

🟡 Room 2: Planet Verb (Digit: 3)

# Sentence Correct Answer Why It’s Correct
1 The dog ___ over the fence. B) jumped Jumped shows action — what the dog did.
2 She ___ a beautiful song at the concert. C) sang Sang shows action — what she did.
3 The flowers ___ in the spring garden. A) bloom Bloom shows action — what the flowers do.
4 My brother ___ his homework every night. C) finishes Finishes shows action — what the brother does.

Common Mistakes: - Choosing nouns that seem “active” (e.g., “concert” sounds exciting but isn’t a verb) - Missing irregular verb forms like “sang” (past tense of “sing”) - Confusing “bloom” — students may think it’s a noun (a bloom), but here it’s used as a verb

🟢 Room 3: Planet Adjective (Digit: 1)

# Sentence Correct Answer Why It’s Correct
1 The ___ kitten played with yarn. B) tiny Tiny describes the kitten — what kind of kitten? A tiny one.
2 We ate ___ pizza for dinner. C) delicious Delicious describes the pizza — what kind?
3 The ___ castle stood on the hill. A) ancient Ancient describes the castle — what kind?
4 She wore a ___ dress to the party. B) sparkly Sparkly describes the dress — what kind?
5 Three ___ birds sat on the wire. C) colorful Colorful describes the birds — what kind?

Common Mistakes: - Confusing adjectives with adverbs (both describe, but adjectives describe NOUNS) - Missing that numbers can be adjectives (“three” in Q5 is also an adjective, but “colorful” is the target) - Choosing the noun instead of the adjective — remind students adjectives DESCRIBE the noun

🔵 Room 4: Planet Adverb (Digit: 9)

# Sentence Correct Answer Why It’s Correct
1 The rabbit hopped ___ across the yard. B) quickly Quickly tells HOW the rabbit hopped. Ends in -ly.
2 She ___ finished her test before anyone else. A) easily Easily tells HOW she finished. Ends in -ly.
3 The baby slept ___ in the crib. C) peacefully Peacefully tells HOW the baby slept. Ends in -ly.
4 He will arrive ___. B) tomorrow Tomorrow tells WHEN he will arrive. (Doesn’t end in -ly!)

Common Mistakes: - Students rely too heavily on the “-ly” rule and miss Q4 (tomorrow) - Confusing adverbs with adjectives — remind: adverbs describe VERBS (how/when/where the action happens) - Choosing “will” in Q4 — it’s a helping verb, not an adverb

🟣 Room 5: Planet Sentences (Digit: 5)

# Sentence Correct Answer Why It’s Correct
1 “The cat sat on the mat.” A) Complete Sentence Has subject (cat), verb (sat), complete thought. ✅
2 “Running through the field.” B) Fragment No subject — WHO is running? Missing! ❌
3 “Because it was raining outside.” B) Fragment Has subject + verb BUT starts with “because” — dependent clause, not a complete thought. ❌
4 “My teacher reads to us every day.” A) Complete Sentence Has subject (teacher), verb (reads), complete thought. ✅
5 “The big, scary, green.” B) Fragment Just adjectives + article — no verb, no complete thought. ❌

Common Mistakes: - Q3 is the trickiest — students see a subject (“it”) and verb (“was raining”) and think it’s complete. Teach that “because” makes it a dependent clause that needs more information. - Q5 — students may think adjectives alone make a sentence. Ask: “What about the big, scary, green WHAT? What did it DO?” - Q2 — “-ing” words feel like verbs but “running” alone (without a helper like “is” or “was”) doesn’t make a complete predicate.
