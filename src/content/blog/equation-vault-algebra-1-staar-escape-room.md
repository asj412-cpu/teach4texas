## The TEKS that matter in an Algebra I EOC review block

Talk about them the way you plan, not as a standards dump.

**TEKS A.5A** is solving linear equations in one variable, including those that need the distributive property and variables on both sides. Kids who can clear \(2x + 5 = 17\) still freeze when the stem wraps parentheses around a binomial or puts \(x\) on both sides. Solve means finish the equation — not label the family.

**TEKS A.9C** is writing exponential functions in the form \(f(x) = ab^x\) (where \(b\) is rational) for mathematical and real-world situations, including growth and decay. The move that kills a naming deck is *write*. Identifying “this looks exponential” is not A.9C. Building \(a\) and \(b\) from a table, story, or percent change is.

**TEKS A.8A** is solving quadratic equations having real solutions by factoring, taking square roots, completing the square, and applying the quadratic formula. One method forever is not enough. STAAR will rotate the method the stem wants — or leave the method open and punish kids who only know factoring.

Those three travel together in a vault format because stations can specialize, then a boss mixes them the way an EOC set does. A single “which function family?” worksheet cannot carry that arc.

## Classroom pitfalls that look like “we did equations”

**Lock-icon trivia as the whole review.** Matching linear / quadratic / exponential next to a cartoon padlock keeps the room quiet. It does not practice A.5A distribution under mild pressure, A.9C writing \(f(x) = ab^x\), or A.8A method flexibility. Save the naming warm-up for the first three minutes — do not let it eat the block.

**Elementary cadet energy in Algebra I.** Cute clipart and bare \(3x = 12\) stems signal the wrong assessment. Algebra I EOC is a high-school course exam (often taken in Grade 8 or 9). The room should look operational — lab operators, not kindergarten mascots.

**Calculator off because “escape rooms don’t use them.”** Algebra I STAAR EOC expects a graphing calculator. If your review bans calculators so the lock math feels harder, you are rehearsing the wrong test.

**One quadratic method only.** Teachers search “write equations” and still get factoring-only decks that never touch square roots, completing the square, or the quadratic formula. EOC does not promise you a clean silo. Mix the methods.

**Stations that never write an exponential.** Identifying a growth curve is not A.9C. If September never forces kids to build \(a\) and \(b\) from a situation, spring CFA will feel like a different sport.

## What a good Algebra I equation vault looks like

Keep setup light enough for a 45–60 minute block or a two-day station split.

1. **Force-copy Google Slides + Present mode.** Kids play their own copy. You keep the teacher master. No editor-share chaos.
2. **Pairs with a talk job.** “Where did the parentheses open? What is \(a\) and what is \(b\)? Which method fits this quadratic?”
3. **Chips that build a short code.** Correct work earns progress. Wrong answers loop with a hint, not a lecture from you at every desk.
4. **Self-checking slides plus a paper tracker.** Pencil work keeps kids honest about the equation they wrote and the method they chose.
5. **A mixed boss at the end.** A.5A, A.9C, and A.8A in one finish — the closest thing to how EOC stacks skills.

Linear Gate → Reactor Bay → Impact Chamber → Diagnostic Dock → GRID boss is a clean review arc.
