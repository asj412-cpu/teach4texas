Texas Grade 4 morning work has the same quiet failure mode as Grade 3 — just with harder TEKS. The tub looks full. Kids are “busy.” You get ten minutes to take attendance, check notes, and start the real block. Then Thursday’s CFA asks a **4.4D** multi-digit multiply or a **4.11C** revise-for-clarity, and the room acts like those skills never showed up before 8:15.

That is what busywork morning work does. It occupies. It does not rehearse the TEKS you are about to teach.

If your packet could be sold in any state with the Texas title sticker swapped on, you already know the gap. Common Core morning work is not a Texas warmup. A coloring sheet with a lone star in the corner is not a TEKS warmup. An escape room is not morning work — it eats the block.

The real ask: **10–15 minutes**, mixed **Math + RLA**, every page labeled with the Texas TEKS you can defend in a PLC, printable when the carts are in someone else’s hallway, digital when you need Easel.

## The TEKS that should be visible before the bell work is done

A four-week set will rotate, but Week 1 of a serious Grade 4 pack should already look like Texas — not a generic “back to school” packet.

**TEKS 4.2B** is representing the value of a digit in whole numbers through 1,000,000,000 and decimals to the hundredths using expanded notation and numerals. Morning work that never asks them to expand or rename a place is not warming 4.2B.

**TEKS 4.4A** is adding and subtracting whole numbers and decimals to the hundredths place. If the warmup never asks them to line up place values in a short story, you are filling boxes.

**TEKS 4.4D** is multiplying up to a four-digit number by a one-digit number and two-digit by two-digit using strategies and the standard algorithm. One tight multiply with a reason beats a wall of isolated products.

**TEKS 4.5B** is input-output tables and numerical expressions to generate a number pattern that follows a given rule. Kids who “got” the story still misread the columns. If morning work never touches a table, your math block owns the whole recovery.

On the RLA side, name the standard on the page.

**TEKS 4.3B** is using context within and beyond a sentence to determine the meaning of unfamiliar words. A vocab box with no sentence is not 4.3B.

**TEKS 4.6G** is evaluating details to determine key ideas. Short text. One job. Not a novel study before announcements.

**TEKS 4.11C** is revising drafts for clarity and coherence. **TEKS 4.11D** is editing drafts for conventions. STAAR does not ask them to circle a noun in isolation at 7:55. It asks them to fix the draft.

Those eight are a sample Week 1 map, not a year-long scope. The point is visibility. If you cannot point to the TEKS code on the student page, it is busywork wearing a Texas hat.

## What a useful Grade 4 morning pack actually does

1. **Names the TEKS on the student page.** Not only in the teacher key.
2. **Mixes Math + RLA every day.** Attendance time is not a single-subject silo.
3. **Prints for tubs and centers, Easel when you need digital response.** Same items, two delivery modes.
4. **Answer key you can scan from the door.** Morning work that needs a 20-minute debrief is a lesson, not a warmup.
5. **Kid-safe Cadet Corps tone.** Texas energy without turning 8:00 into a lock-and-code production.

Four days a week, four weeks, is an honest first set: **16 days**. Enough to prove the routine before you buy another month of filler.
