# 🔍 Detective Grammar — Teacher Guide
## Escape Room 2 | 4th Grade | 30–45 Minutes

---

## 📋 Overview
Students solve a mystery by visiting 5 crime scenes and fixing grammar errors to collect evidence letters. Unscramble the letters to reveal the thief's name.

**TEKS:** 4.11D.vi (pronouns), 4.11D.ix (punctuation including commas)

**Skills:** Subject-verb agreement, pronoun-antecedent agreement, comma usage

---

## 🖥️ How to Assign in Google Classroom
1. Open Google Classroom → Classwork → **+ Create** → **Assignment**
2. Attach the Google Slides file → **"Make a copy for each student"**
3. Instructions: *"Work through each crime scene in order. Record your evidence letters on the Evidence Tracker (Slide 3). Unscramble the letters at the end to catch the thief!"*
4. Assign!

| Setup | Activity | Wrap-Up |
|-------|----------|---------|
| 5 min | 25–35 min | 5–10 min |

**Wrap-Up Suggestion:** Review Scene 5 (mixed review) together — it's the hardest and generates the best discussion.

---

## 🎯 Differentiation

### Struggling Learners:
- Provide a grammar rules reference sheet (SVA, pronouns, commas)
- Allow partner work
- Focus on Scenes 1, 3, and 4 (skip Scene 2 which has tricky SVA)

### Advanced Learners:
- Challenge: Find additional errors the detective "missed" (write their own)
- Time challenge: Complete in under 25 minutes
- Write a "Case Report" paragraph using correct SVA, pronouns, and commas

### ELL Students:
- Pre-teach: subject, verb, pronoun, antecedent, comma
- Allow native language grammar reference
- Pair with bilingual buddy

---

## 🏫 Center vs. Whole Class

### Center: 
- 1–2 rotation periods
- Self-directed, minimal teacher support needed
- Print Evidence Tracker for paper option

### Whole Class:
- Project and discuss after each scene
- Great for grammar review before assessments
- Use "think-pair-share" for each challenge

---

## 🎮 Extension Activities
1. **Write the News Article:** Students write a Grammar City Gazette article about the thief being caught — must use correct SVA, pronouns, and commas
2. **Create a New Crime Scene:** Students write their own Scene 6 with 4 grammar questions
3. **Pronoun Swap:** Give students a paragraph and have them replace nouns with correct pronouns
4. **Comma Surgery:** Give students unpunctuated paragraphs to add commas

---

## 📊 Assessment
- Formative assessment for subject-verb agreement, pronoun usage, and comma rules
- Great pre-test/post-test activity for grammar unit
- Works as STAAR grammar prep (4th grade conventions)
