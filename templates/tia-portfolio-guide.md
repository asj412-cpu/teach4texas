# Building Your TIA Portfolio: A Step-by-Step Guide for Texas Teachers

Your TIA designation is built on evidence. The strongest candidates don't scramble to assemble their case at the end of the year — they build it systematically from Day 1. This guide gives you the exact framework to do that.

## What Is a TIA Portfolio?

Your TIA portfolio is the organized collection of evidence that supports your designation. While your district's formal TIA system uses specific data points (STAAR growth percentiles, T-TESS observation scores), a well-built portfolio goes beyond the minimum. It tells the complete story of your effectiveness and gives you a personal archive to reference during pre/post conferences, goal-setting meetings, and future designation cycles.

**Important:** Your district's TIA system determines what officially counts. This portfolio guide helps you organize and go beyond the basics — check your district's requirements first.

---

## Portfolio Structure

Organize your portfolio into these sections:

### Section 1: Professional Summary
- One-page overview: your teaching context, years of experience, grade/subject, campus demographics
- Your professional goals for the year (aligned to TIA criteria)
- Your current designation status or target designation

### Section 2: Student Growth Evidence (Pillar 1)
- Beginning-of-year diagnostic data (baseline)
- Unit pre/post assessment data with growth calculations
- Mid-year benchmark results
- STAAR progress data (when available)
- Student growth tracker summaries (class-wide and individual)
- Student goal-setting artifacts
- Intervention logs and results

### Section 3: Observation Evidence (Pillar 2)
- T-TESS observation scores and feedback
- Pre-conference planning documents
- Post-conference reflections
- Lesson plans demonstrating Distinguished-level practices
- Video clips of instruction (if your district allows/encourages)
- Self-assessment against T-TESS rubric

### Section 4: Supporting Evidence
- Professional development log with implementation evidence
- Leadership and mentoring activities
- Family/community engagement log
- Peer observation notes (observing others and being observed)
- Student work samples showing growth over time
- Letters or feedback from administrators, colleagues, or families

### Section 5: Reflections and Goals
- End-of-year reflection narrative
- Growth areas identified and plans for next year
- Updated professional goals for the next designation cycle

## Evidence Collection Strategies

### For Student Growth (Pillar 1)

**Use a consistent assessment format.** When your pre and post assessments use the same format and rigor level, growth is easy to calculate and defend. Create or adopt a standard assessment template for your grade/subject.

**Track at the student level, not just the class level.** TIA cares about individual student growth. Your tracker should show each student's trajectory, not just class averages.

**Save the raw data AND the analysis.** Keep the actual assessment scores in a spreadsheet, but also write brief analyses: "14 of 22 students showed growth of 20%+ on fraction operations. 3 students showed minimal growth — all three had attendance below 80% during the unit."

**Document interventions.** When a student isn't growing, document what you did about it: pulled for small group, modified materials, contacted family, referred to RTI. This shows you respond to data, not just collect it.

**Collect student voice.** Student reflections on their own growth ("I used to struggle with... but now I can...") are powerful portfolio additions.

### For Observations (Pillar 2)

**Don't wait for formal observations.** Ask your evaluator or a peer to do informal walk-throughs and provide feedback using the T-TESS rubric. Add this to your portfolio.

**Annotate your lesson plans.** After teaching, add margin notes: "This worked because..." or "Next time I'd change..." This shows reflective practice.

**Keep a "Distinguished Moments" log.** When something in your instruction goes exceptionally well — a student has a breakthrough, a discussion reaches unexpected depth, a differentiation strategy clicks — jot it down that day. You'll forget by May.

**Video yourself.** Even if your district doesn't require it, recording yourself teaches you more than any PD session. Watch 10 minutes of your instruction and score yourself on the T-TESS rubric. Put your self-assessments in the portfolio.

## Month-by-Month Evidence Collection Calendar

### August: Set the Foundation
- [ ] Review your district's TIA system requirements and scoring thresholds
- [ ] Set professional goals aligned to TIA criteria (write them down)
- [ ] Create or set up your student growth tracking system
- [ ] Administer beginning-of-year diagnostics in all classes
- [ ] Document baseline data in your portfolio
- [ ] Take photos of your classroom setup (learning environment evidence)

### September: Establish Baselines
- [ ] Complete first unit pre/post assessment cycle
- [ ] Calculate and record growth data from first unit
- [ ] Hold first student goal-setting conferences
- [ ] Begin data wall or tracking system updates
- [ ] Submit your first annotated lesson plan to portfolio
- [ ] Schedule or request an informal walk-through observation

### October: Build Momentum
- [ ] Complete second unit pre/post cycle
- [ ] Update student growth tracker
- [ ] Document a differentiation strategy with evidence of impact
- [ ] Collect first round of student work samples (select 3-5 representative students to track all year)
- [ ] Log professional development attended and implementation plans
- [ ] Begin intervention documentation for students showing limited growth

### November: Mid-Fall Check-In
- [ ] Analyze cumulative growth data (BOY to now)
- [ ] Identify students on track and students needing additional support
- [ ] Document parent/family engagement efforts
- [ ] Complete a self-assessment on T-TESS rubric — identify Distinguished targets
- [ ] Add leadership/mentoring evidence to portfolio
- [ ] Prepare for formal observation if scheduled this semester

### December: Semester 1 Wrap-Up
- [ ] Administer semester benchmark or cumulative assessment
- [ ] Calculate semester growth data
- [ ] Write a brief Semester 1 reflection (1 page): what's working, what to adjust
- [ ] Update student goal-setting data
- [ ] Organize portfolio sections — fill any gaps
- [ ] Review observation feedback received this semester

### January: Reset and Refocus
- [ ] Set Semester 2 goals based on Semester 1 data
- [ ] Hold updated student goal-setting conferences
- [ ] Adjust intervention groups based on current data
- [ ] Plan for any remaining formal observations
- [ ] Document any new PD or leadership activities
- [ ] Add mid-year student work samples to portfolio

### February: Intensify
- [ ] Ramp up spiral review and cumulative practice
- [ ] Complete unit pre/post cycles (continue documenting)
- [ ] Conduct practice STAAR or benchmark assessment
- [ ] Analyze practice data — document findings and instructional response
- [ ] Add observation evidence from any February walk-throughs
- [ ] Update growth tracker with current data

### March: Final Push
- [ ] Targeted intervention for students close to growth thresholds
- [ ] Error analysis activities with students using practice test data
- [ ] Document instructional adjustments made based on recent data
- [ ] Collect student reflections on their growth journey
- [ ] Ensure all formal observations are completed
- [ ] Begin drafting end-of-year reflection narrative

### April: Assessment Window
- [ ] Support students through STAAR/assessment period
- [ ] Document your test prep approach and strategies used
- [ ] Continue non-STAAR pre/post cycles for ongoing units
- [ ] Collect final student work samples for tracked students
- [ ] Add any remaining professional evidence to portfolio

### May: Compile and Reflect
- [ ] Gather final assessment/STAAR data when available
- [ ] Calculate full-year growth data (BOY to EOY)
- [ ] Complete end-of-year reflection narrative
- [ ] Compile final student work samples showing year-long growth
- [ ] Organize complete portfolio — all five sections
- [ ] Set preliminary goals for next year
- [ ] Back up your portfolio digitally (Google Drive, external drive, etc.)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Waiting Until Spring to Collect Evidence
By far the most common mistake. If you start building your portfolio in March, you've lost 7 months of evidence. The calendar above exists for a reason — start in August.

### 2. Collecting Data Without Analysis
A spreadsheet full of numbers isn't evidence. Every data point in your portfolio should include a brief analysis: What does this show? What did you do about it? How did students respond?

### 3. Focusing on Only One Pillar
Some teachers obsess over student growth data and neglect their observation scores, or vice versa. TIA evaluates both. Balance your effort.

### 4. Not Knowing Your District's System
Every district's TIA system is different. If you don't know the exact scoring thresholds, weightings, and evidence requirements for your district, you're guessing. Find out first, then build your portfolio accordingly.

### 5. Keeping Evidence Only on Paper
Paper gets lost, damaged, or disorganized. Maintain a digital portfolio (Google Drive folder, shared drive, etc.) with everything scanned or digital. Keep a backup.

### 6. Treating Portfolio as Busywork
If you're collecting evidence just to check boxes, it shows. The best portfolios are organic extensions of what you're already doing — they document practices you'd do regardless of TIA.

### 7. Not Including Student Voice
Student growth data is numbers. Student reflections bring those numbers to life. "I used to hate math because I didn't understand fractions. Now I can teach my little sister." That's more powerful than any percentile.

### 8. Skipping the Reflection
Data without reflection is incomplete. Your end-of-year narrative should demonstrate that you don't just produce results — you understand why those results happened and how to replicate or improve them.

## Quick-Reference Checklist: TIA Portfolio Essentials

Print this and check items off as you collect them.

### Student Growth (Pillar 1)
- [ ] Beginning-of-year diagnostic data (all classes)
- [ ] Unit pre/post assessment data (minimum 4-6 units)
- [ ] Growth calculations (individual and class-wide)
- [ ] Mid-year benchmark data
- [ ] STAAR progress data (if applicable)
- [ ] Student growth tracking spreadsheet (updated regularly)
- [ ] Student goal-setting conference notes/artifacts
- [ ] Intervention logs with outcomes
- [ ] Student reflections on their growth
- [ ] Year-end growth summary with analysis

### Observations (Pillar 2)
- [ ] All formal T-TESS observation scores and written feedback
- [ ] Pre-conference planning documents
- [ ] Post-conference reflection notes
- [ ] 3-5 exemplary lesson plans (annotated with data connections)
- [ ] Self-assessment against T-TESS rubric (at least twice per year)
- [ ] Informal walk-through feedback (if available)
- [ ] Video of instruction (optional but powerful)

### Supporting Evidence
- [ ] Professional development log (with implementation notes)
- [ ] Leadership/mentoring documentation
- [ ] Family engagement log (contacts, conferences, events)
- [ ] Student work samples from tracked students (BOY, MOY, EOY)
- [ ] Professional goal documentation (goals set, progress, outcomes)
- [ ] End-of-year reflection narrative (1-2 pages)

## Final Advice

Your TIA portfolio isn't a scrapbook. It's a strategic evidence file that tells the story of your impact. Every piece should answer one question: **"How does this demonstrate that I'm an effective teacher who grows students?"**

If an item doesn't answer that question, leave it out. If it does, document it clearly with context and analysis.

The teachers who earn the highest TIA designations aren't necessarily the ones who work the hardest. They're the ones who work intentionally — with data, with purpose, and with documentation.

Start building your portfolio today. Your future self (and your future paycheck) will thank you.
