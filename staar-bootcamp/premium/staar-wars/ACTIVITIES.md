# ⭐ STAAR Wars — Themed Activities

> 12 ready-to-run activities for your STAAR Wars Boot Camp week. Mix of individual, partner, and group. Includes 3 review games.

---

## 🎮 REVIEW GAMES

### 1. Asteroid Field (Whole Class Game)
**Type:** Group | **Time:** 20-25 min | **Best for:** Any subject review

**Setup:**
- Create 20-30 review questions (STAAR format) on index cards or slides
- Divide class into 3-4 "spacecraft crews" (teams)
- Draw a simple space grid on the whiteboard (5x5) — some squares have asteroids (X), some are clear

**How to Play:**
1. Teams take turns answering questions
2. Correct answer = team moves one space forward on the grid
3. If they land on an asteroid, they lose a turn (but stay in place)
4. If they get a question wrong, they don't move
5. First team to cross the grid "escapes the asteroid field"

**Bonus:** Add "wormhole" squares that jump the team ahead 2 spaces, and "black hole" squares that send them back 1.

**Materials:** Index cards or slides with questions, whiteboard, markers, magnets or sticky notes for team markers

---

### 2. Warp Speed Challenge (Fast-Paced Whole Class)
**Type:** Individual competitive | **Time:** 15-20 min | **Best for:** Quick fact review, computation, vocabulary

**Setup:**
- Project questions one at a time (5-10 second timer per question)
- Students write answers on whiteboards

**How to Play:**
1. Display question + start timer
2. Students write answer and hold up whiteboard when timer ends
3. Correct answers earn 1 point (students track their own score)
4. Every 5 questions, announce who's in "warp speed" (5/5 correct)
5. Final score: students who got 80%+ earn a "Warp Speed Badge"

**Twist:** Include 2-3 "Hyperdrive" questions worth double points (harder questions).

**Materials:** Projector/slides, whiteboards, markers, timer

---

### 3. Escape the Space Station (Escape Room Style)
**Type:** Small group (3-4 students) | **Time:** 30-40 min | **Best for:** Mixed subject review

**Setup:**
- Create 5 "locks" (envelopes or folders) each containing a puzzle/question set
- Each lock has 4-5 STAAR-format questions
- Correct answers reveal a code (e.g., the answer to #3 is "B" — B=2, so the code digit is 2)
- Final code opens the "airlock" (a sealed envelope with a prize or certificate inside)

**How to Play:**
1. Groups start at Lock 1
2. Solve all questions correctly to get the code for that lock
3. Bring code to teacher to verify, then get Lock 2
4. First group to crack all 5 codes and open the airlock wins
5. All groups that finish within the time limit earn "Escape Specialist" badges

**Materials:** 5 envelopes per group (labeled Lock 1–5), question sets, code answer key, "airlock" prize envelope, timer

## 👤 INDIVIDUAL ACTIVITIES

### 4. Mission Log Journal (Daily Writing)
**Type:** Individual | **Time:** 10-15 min | **Best for:** ELA/writing practice + metacognition

**Instructions:**
Each day, students write a "Mission Log" entry in their journal (a folded piece of paper or composition book). Prompts:
- **Monday:** "Mission Log Day 1: Today I learned/reviewed... The skill I need to work on most is... because..."
- **Tuesday:** "Mission Log Day 2: My biggest math challenge is... Here's how I plan to tackle it..."
- **Wednesday:** "Mission Log Day 3: Something that surprised me in science/social studies review was..."
- **Thursday:** "Mission Log Day 4: During today's simulation, I felt... My strategy for tough questions is..."
- **Friday:** "Mission Log Day 5: I am ready for STAAR because... My advice to a nervous cadet would be..."

**Materials:** Paper or journals, pencils

---

### 5. Star Map Concept Organizer (Individual Study Tool)
**Type:** Individual | **Time:** 20 min | **Best for:** Organizing knowledge in any subject

**Instructions:**
Students draw a large star with 5 points on a piece of paper. In the center: the subject name. On each point of the star, they write one major concept/topic. Along each line between points, they write connections between concepts. This creates a visual "star map" of their knowledge.

**Example (5th Grade Science):**
- Center: "Science"
- Points: Matter & Energy, Force & Motion, Earth & Space, Organisms, Ecosystems
- Lines: "Energy can change forms of matter" (between Matter & Force), "Organisms depend on ecosystems" (between Organisms & Ecosystems)

**Materials:** Large paper (11x17 or butcher paper), colored markers

---

### 6. Cadet Training Flashcards (Self-Study)
**Type:** Individual | **Time:** 15-20 min to create, then used throughout the week | **Best for:** Vocabulary, key facts, formulas

**Instructions:**
Students create themed flashcards:
- Front: Term/concept with a small space-themed doodle
- Back: Definition, example, and a memory trick
- Cards are stored in a "Mission Pouch" (ziplock bag decorated with stars)
- Students quiz themselves for 5 minutes at the start of each Training Block

**Materials:** Index cards, markers, ziplock bags, star stickers

## 👥 PARTNER ACTIVITIES

### 7. Co-Pilot Quiz (Partner Quiz)
**Type:** Partner | **Time:** 20 min | **Best for:** Any subject review

**Instructions:**
1. Each student writes 5 STAAR-style questions based on the day's topic (with answer key)
2. Partners swap questions
3. Each partner completes the other's quiz
4. Partners grade each other and discuss any disagreements
5. "Co-Pilot Rating": Both partners must agree on all answers — if they disagree, they must find evidence to resolve it

**Materials:** Paper, pencils, textbook/notes for reference

---

### 8. Mission Control & Pilot (Problem Solving Pairs)
**Type:** Partner | **Time:** 20-25 min | **Best for:** Math problem solving, multi-step questions

**Instructions:**
- Partner A = Pilot (solves the problem, thinking aloud)
- Partner B = Mission Control (listens, asks questions, checks work — but does NOT solve)
- Mission Control can say: "Check your calculation," "What's the question asking?," "Can you explain that step?"
- After 2-3 problems, switch roles

**Why it works:** Forces students to articulate their thinking. Mission Control catches errors the Pilot misses.

**Materials:** Problem set (6-8 multi-step problems), pencils, scratch paper

## 👥👥 GROUP ACTIVITIES

### 9. Planetary Stations (Rotation Activity)
**Type:** Groups of 4-5 | **Time:** 30-40 min | **Best for:** Multi-topic review

**Setup:**
Set up 4-5 stations around the room, each labeled as a planet:
- **Mercury** — Quick computation/grammar (fast and close to the sun)
- **Mars** — Reading comprehension (the red planet = read!)
- **Jupiter** — Word problems (the biggest challenges)
- **Saturn** — Vocabulary/key terms (rings = connections)
- **Neptune** — Science/social studies (the far reaches)

**How to Play:**
1. Groups rotate every 6-8 minutes
2. At each planet, complete the task and record answers on a "Flight Log" sheet
3. Groups that complete all planets correctly earn "Galaxy Explorer" status

**Materials:** Station question sets, Flight Log recording sheets, timer, planet labels for stations

---

### 10. Intergalactic Debate (Structured Discussion)
**Type:** Groups of 4 | **Time:** 20 min | **Best for:** Reading/ELA, Social Studies, constructed response practice

**Instructions:**
1. Present a STAAR-style question with two plausible answer choices
2. Within each group, 2 students argue for Choice A, 2 for Choice B
3. Each side has 2 minutes to present their evidence (from the text/source)
4. After both sides present, the group votes on the correct answer
5. Teacher reveals the correct answer and discusses why

**Example:** "Which sentence BEST describes the author's purpose?" — show two reasonable options. Students must use textual evidence to argue their case.

**Materials:** Debate question prompts (3-4 per session), passages/sources, timer

---

### 11. Space Station Build (Collaborative Study Guide)
**Type:** Groups of 3-4 | **Time:** 25-30 min | **Best for:** End-of-topic synthesis

**Instructions:**
Each group is assigned a "section" of the space station to build (a piece of butcher paper). Their section covers one major topic/reporting category. Groups must include:
- 5 key vocabulary words with definitions
- 3 example problems/questions with solutions
- 2 test-taking tips specific to that topic
- 1 visual/diagram

Completed sections are posted on the wall to form a complete "space station" that serves as a class study guide.

**Materials:** Butcher paper or poster board, markers, textbooks/notes for reference, tape

---

### 12. Alien Transmission Decoder (Problem Relay)
**Type:** Teams of 4-5 | **Time:** 15-20 min | **Best for:** Math computation, grammar/mechanics

**Instructions:**
1. Each team lines up facing the board
2. First person runs to the board, grabs a question from the "transmission" (envelope), solves it, writes the answer
3. Runs back and tags next teammate
4. Next teammate grabs question 2 and solves it
5. Continue until all questions are answered
6. First team to correctly decode all transmissions wins

**Catch:** If any answer is wrong, the whole transmission is "garbled" — team must find and fix the error before it counts.

**Materials:** Envelopes with individual question slips (5-6 per team), answer recording sheets, markers
