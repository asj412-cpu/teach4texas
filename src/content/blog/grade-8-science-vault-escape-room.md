## The TEKS that matter in a Grade 8 science review block

Talk about them the way you plan, not as a standards dump.

**TEKS 8.6E** is investigating how mass is conserved in chemical reactions and relating that to the rearrangement of atoms using chemical equations, **including photosynthesis**. In the room that means: can they read a closed-system story, count atoms on both sides, and still catch the photosynthesis equation when it shows up mixed with a mass question?

**TEKS 8.7A** is calculating and analyzing how acceleration depends on net force and mass using Newton’s Second Law. Kids who “get” \(F = ma\) still freeze when the stem asks for mass, or when they have to explain why doubling mass halves acceleration if force stays put. Calculate and analyze travel together.

**TEKS 8.7B** is investigating and describing how Newton’s **three** laws act **simultaneously** in systems — restraints, sports, rides, tectonics, rockets. The word that kills the trivia deck is *simultaneously*. One law named is not 8.7B.

Those three travel together in a vault format because stations can specialize, then a boss mixes them the way a STAAR set does. A single “which law is this?” worksheet cannot carry that arc.

## Classroom pitfalls that look like “we did Newton”

**Lock-icon trivia as the whole review.** Naming a law next to a cartoon padlock keeps the room quiet. It does not practice 8.6E atom counts, 8.7A calculation under mild pressure, or 8.7B simultaneous laws in one system. Save the naming warm-up for the first three minutes — do not let it eat the block.

**Elementary cadet energy in Grade 8.** Cute clipart and “which law?” baby stems signal the wrong assessment. Grade 8 Science is the middle-school science test administered in Grade 8. The room should look operational — lab operators, not kindergarten mascots.

**Calculator off because “escape rooms don’t use them.”** Grade 8 Science STAAR expects access to a basic four-function device. If your review bans calculators so the lock math feels harder, you are rehearsing the wrong test.

**Skipping photosynthesis inside 8.6E.** Teachers search “science 8.6E” and still get mass-only decks that never touch the photosynthesis equation. STAAR does not promise you a clean silo. Mix it.

**One law per station forever.** Stations that only isolate First, Second, and Third never force the 8.7B move: all three at once inside seatbelts, a launch, or a ride. If September never touches simultaneous laws, spring CFA will feel like a different sport.

## What a good Grade 8 Science vault looks like

Keep setup light enough for a 45–60 minute block or a two-day station split.

1. **Force-copy Google Slides + Present mode.** Kids play their own copy. You keep the teacher master. No editor-share chaos.
2. **Pairs with a talk job.** “What stays the same on both sides of the equation? What is the stem asking for — \(a\), \(F\), or \(m\)?”
3. **Chips that build a short code.** Correct work earns progress. Wrong answers loop with a hint, not a lecture from you at every desk.
4. **Self-checking slides plus a paper tracker.** Pencil work keeps kids honest about the calculation they wrote and the laws they claimed were acting together.
5. **A mixed boss at the end.** 8.6E, 8.7A, and 8.7B in one finish — the closest thing to how STAAR stacks skills.

Mass Lock → Acceleration Bay → Body-force Chamber → Systems Dock → LABS boss is a clean review arc.
