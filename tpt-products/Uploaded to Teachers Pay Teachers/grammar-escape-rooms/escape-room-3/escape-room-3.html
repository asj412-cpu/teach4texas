🏝️ Grammar Island Survivor — Teacher Guide

Escape Room 3 | 5th Grade | 30–45 Minutes

📋 Overview

Students are stranded on Grammar Island and must solve 5 grammar challenges to earn raft pieces and escape. Covers complex sentences, conjunctions, prepositional phrases, and irregular past tense verbs.

TEKS: 5.11D.vii (subordinating conjunctions), 5.11D.ii (past tense of irregular verbs)

🖥️ How to Assign in Google Classroom

  1. Classwork → + Create → Assignment
  2. Attach Google Slides → “Make a copy for each student”
  3. Instructions: “You’re stranded on Grammar Island! Solve 5 grammar challenges to build your escape raft. Record your coordinates on the Raft Tracker (Slide 3).”

⏰ Timing

Setup Activity Wrap-Up
5 min 30–40 min 5 min

This escape room is slightly more challenging (5th grade) — allow full 45 minutes for struggling learners.

🎯 Differentiation

Struggling Learners:

  • Provide a conjunction reference chart (FANBOYS + subordinating list)
  • Allow partner work
  • Pre-teach irregular verb forms with a quick sort activity
  • Skip Challenge 5 (mixed review) if needed — award the coordinate freely

Advanced Learners:

  • Write a “Survivor Journal” using all 4 grammar skills in a paragraph
  • Create Challenge 6 with their own questions
  • Identify MULTIPLE prepositional phrases per sentence in Challenge 3
  • Time challenge: escape in under 20 minutes

ELL Students:

  • Irregular verbs are especially challenging for ELLs — provide a past tense verb chart
  • Pre-teach prepositions with physical demonstrations (put the book ON, UNDER, BESIDE)
  • Allow use of bilingual resources

🏫 Center vs. Whole Class

Center:

  • 1–2 rotation periods
  • Pair struggling/advanced students strategically
  • Provide printed conjunction/preposition reference at the center

Whole Class:

  • Work through Challenge 1 together as a model
  • Release students for independent work on Challenges 2–5
  • Debrief Challenge 5 together (mixed review generates great discussion)

🎮 Extension Activities

  1. Island Journal: Write a 3-paragraph survival story using at least 5 subordinating conjunctions, 5 prepositional phrases, and 5 irregular past tense verbs
  2. Conjunction Sort: Cut-and-sort activity separating coordinating from subordinating conjunctions
  3. Irregular Verb Memory Match: Create cards matching present to past tense
  4. Prepositional Phrase Hunt: Find 10 prepositional phrases in a chapter book

📊 Assessment

  • Strong formative assessment for 5th grade grammar conventions
  • Excellent STAAR prep for grammar/editing questions
  • Use Challenge 5 (mixed review) scores to identify which skills need reteaching

🏝️ Grammar Island Survivor — Answer Key

Escape Room 3 | 5th Grade

Escape Coordinates: N-3-7-W-2

🪵 Challenge 1: Log Lagoon — Subordinating Conjunctions (Coordinate: N)

# Sentence Correct Explanation
1 ___ the sun went down, the temperature dropped. After Shows time sequence — one event after another.
2 We can’t go swimming ___ the lifeguard arrives. until Condition that must be met before the action can happen.
3 ___ she studied all night, she still felt nervous. Although Shows contrast — studying SHOULD help, but she’s still nervous.
4 The dog hides under the bed ___ there is thunder. whenever Shows a repeated condition — every time there’s thunder.
5 You should wear a jacket ___ it is cold outside. since Shows reason/cause — because it’s cold.

Common Mistakes: Choosing coordinating conjunctions (and, but, or) instead of subordinating ones. Remind students: subordinating conjunctions create DEPENDENT clauses that can’t stand alone.

🧵 Challenge 2: Rope Bridge — FANBOYS / Compound Sentences (Coordinate: 3)

# Sentences Correct Conjunction Explanation
1 Wanted pizza / restaurant closed but Contrast — you wanted something, but couldn’t get it.
2 Practiced every day / made the team so Cause and effect — practicing LED TO making the team.
3 Go to movies / stay home or Choice between two options.
4 Raining hard / still went to park yet Contrast/surprise — despite rain, they went anyway.

Common Mistakes: Confusing “but” and “yet” — both show contrast, but “yet” implies more surprise. Confusing “so” (result) with “and” (addition).

⛵ Challenge 3: Sail Cave — Prepositional Phrases (Coordinate: 7)

# Sentence Prep Phrase Preposition
1 Bird flew over the tall trees over the tall trees over
2 Backpack under the desk under the desk under
3 During the storm, power went out During the storm During
4 Led through the old tunnel through the old tunnel through
5 Ate lunch at the new restaurant at the new restaurant at (also “by the lake”)

Common Mistakes: Including the verb in the phrase (“flew over” is NOT the prepositional phrase — just “over the tall trees”). Students also confuse prepositional phrases with the subject/verb of the sentence.

🗺️ Challenge 4: Map Mountain — Irregular Past Tense (Coordinate: W)

# Verb Correct Past Tense Common Wrong Answer
1 drink drank “drinked” (adding -ed to irregular)
2 bring brought “bringed” or “brang”
3 fly flew “flyed”
4 teach taught “teached”
5 swim swam “swimmed”

Common Mistakes: Adding “-ed” to irregular verbs is the #1 error. Also confusing simple past (drank, swam) with past participle (drunk, swum) — these are different! Simple past stands alone; past participle needs a helper (has/had/have).

🏴 Challenge 5: Flag Fortress — Mixed Review (Coordinate: 2)

# Correct Answer Why Others Are Wrong
1 B) “Although she ran fast, she missed the bus.” A: “runned” isn’t a word. C: missing comma after dependent clause.
2 B) “The book on the shelf was written by my favorite author.” A: “wrote” should be “written” with “was.” C: “in the shelf” — books go ON shelves.
3 B) “Because the game was canceled, we went to the mall.” A: missing comma. C: reverses cause and effect.
4 C) “They had eaten lunch, so they went outside to play.” A: “ate” should be “eaten” with “had.” B: missing comma before “so.”
5 A) “Before the movie started, we bought popcorn at the counter.” B: missing comma. C: “buyed” — “buy” → “bought.”

Common Mistakes: Q2 is tricky because students must check BOTH the verb (“written” vs “wrote”) AND the preposition (“on” vs “in”). Multiple skills combined = harder.
