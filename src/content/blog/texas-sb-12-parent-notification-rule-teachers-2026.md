> **General information, not legal advice.** This post summarizes a new TEA rule. Your district sets the local procedures and forms for following it. Read the official rule linked below and ask your campus administrators how it works on your campus.

Y'all, here's one to read before the turkey leftovers run out. A new TEA rule on telling parents about their child's mental, emotional, and physical health takes effect **November 29, 2026**. It is 19 TAC §103.1105, and it was filed with the Secretary of State on November 9, 2026 ([Texas Register, Nov 20, 2026](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/November202026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html); [TEA adopted rules](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/adopted-commissioner-education-rules-not-yet-effective)).

The rule carries out Senate Bill 12 from the 2025 legislative session ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf); [SB 12 bill history](https://capitol.texas.gov/BillLookup/History.aspx?LegSess=89R&Bill=SB12)). TEA adopted it without changes from the version proposed in June ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)). I first figured this was a counselor-and-nurse rule. It isn't. It applies to all regular staff, so it's worth ten minutes of your week ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

## What changed

The rule sets the steps school districts and open-enrollment charter schools must follow to tell parents about changes in services or monitoring tied to a student's mental, emotional, or physical health or well-being ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

It applies to "personnel," which the rule defines as all employees, plus volunteers and contractors, who interact with students on a regular basis. That includes classroom teachers ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

## The one-school-day rule

This is the part most likely to land on your desk. Personnel must notify a student's parent "within one school day or as soon as feasible" when a student discloses behaviors, including planned behaviors, that represent an adverse change in the student's mental, emotional, or physical health or well-being ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

The rule lists examples but says the list is not limited to them: disclosures related to any sexual behaviors, unlawful use of substances, tattoos or permanent physical or body modifications, violence or suicidal ideation, or other harmful behaviors ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

Several groups, including the Texas Classroom Teachers Association and Texas AFT, asked TEA to delete, narrow, or substantially revise this provision. TEA declined ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

## Check-ins, surveys, and screeners

The rule's definition of a "psychological or psychiatric examination or test" is broad. It covers a method designed to elicit information about an attitude, habit, trait, opinion, belief, or feeling, including one "presented or characterized as a survey, check-in, or screening or is embedded in an academic lesson" ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

A parent must opt in before a student receives a psychological or psychiatric examination or test, unless a specific exemption is provided by law. Parents must also opt in before the school gives a well-being questionnaire or health screening form ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)). And before one of those questionnaires or forms is given, a copy must go to the parent, even when it is scheduled as part of a required course, class, or elective ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

So yes, that mood meter by the door, the feelings survey, or the journal prompt about how kids feel needs a quick check with your campus before you use it ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

## What's still fine

Here's the good news: the rule does not turn every kind word into paperwork. Parental consent is not required to ask a student about their "daily or general well-being" ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

"General caretaking" is not a health-related or health-care service. The rule's examples include cleaning up spills, offering a change of clothes, applying adhesive bandages, wiping a bloody nose, asking about a child's well-being, trying to calm a crying student, and defusing a situation where a student is visibly angry ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

"Monitoring" means planned and recurring observations. Incidental or in-the-moment observations don't count ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)). Academic screenings and services, such as dyslexia screenings and interventions, are not health-related services ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

## When a student shares something

The rule says personnel must either encourage the student to discuss well-being issues with their parent or help facilitate that conversation ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)). District procedures may not encourage students to withhold information from parents ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

Two important exceptions apply. Notice isn't required when a reasonably prudent person would believe telling the parent is likely to result in the student suffering abuse or neglect. If the decision is made not to inform a parent, campus-based personnel must report it to the principal, and the decision must be documented. Behavioral threat assessments follow their own notification rules ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

Nothing in this rule changes your duty to report child abuse or neglect ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)). For that timeline, see our post on [Texas child abuse reporting rules](/blog/texas-child-abuse-reporting-24-hours-tea-rule-2026).

## What this means in your classroom

- **Know your campus process** for a one-school-day notice: who contacts the parent and how. The rule sets the deadline, and it refers to procedures adopted by your school district ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).
- **Keep loving on your kids.** Asking how a student is doing and calming an upset child are still allowed ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).
- **Special education:** services in an IEP, or part of an IDEA eligibility evaluation, follow IDEA's consent rules ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

## What to do this week

1. Ask your principal or counselor for the district's notification procedure before the rule takes effect on November 29 ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/November202026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).
2. Review any recurring check-in routines, surveys, or SEL forms with your campus team ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).
3. Don't encourage a student to keep well-being information from their parent. Nudge them toward that conversation instead ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

## Key dates

- **November 20, 2026:** adoption published in the Texas Register ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/November202026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).
- **November 29, 2026:** rule takes effect ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/November202026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html); [TEA adopted rules](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/adopted-commissioner-education-rules-not-yet-effective)).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When does the SB 12 parent notification rule take effect?

19 TAC §103.1105 takes effect November 29, 2026 ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/November202026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

### Do I need parent permission to ask a student how they're doing?

No. The rule says parental consent is not required to ask about a student's daily or general well-being ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

### What do I have to tell parents, and how fast?

Personnel must notify a parent within one school day or as soon as feasible when a student discloses behaviors that represent an adverse change in the student's mental, emotional, or physical health or well-being ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

### Can I give a feelings survey or check-in in class?

Generally only with parent opt-in. A survey or check-in designed to elicit attitudes, beliefs, or feelings can count as a psychological examination or test, and parents must get a copy of any well-being questionnaire before it is given ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).

### Does this change how I report child abuse?

No. The rule does not alter the duty to report abuse or neglect under the Texas Family Code ([TEA adopted rule](https://tea.texas.gov/laws-and-rules/commissioner-rules-tac/coe-adopted/26-11-103-1105.pdf)).
