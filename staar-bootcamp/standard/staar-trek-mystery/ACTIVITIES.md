# 🔍 STAAR Trek: Mission to Mastery — Themed Activities

> 10 detective-themed activities with 3 review games.

---

## 🎮 REVIEW GAMES

### 1. Interrogation Room (Whole Class)
**Type:** Class game | **Time:** 20 min | **Best for:** Any subject review

**How to Play:**
- Teacher reads a question. The question is the "suspect."
- Students write their answer on whiteboards — this is their "accusation"
- Reveal correct answer. Students who got it right "solved the case" for that round
- Track: How many cases did the class solve out of 15?
- Dramatic narration: "The suspect claims the answer is B. Detective Johnson says C. Let's check the evidence... C IS CORRECT! Case solved!"

**Materials:** Questions, whiteboards, markers

---

### 2. Escape the Crime Scene (Escape Room)
**Type:** Small groups (3-4) | **Time:** 30 min | **Best for:** Mixed review

**Setup:**
- 4-5 sealed envelopes ("evidence bags") per group
- Each contains a question set; correct answers reveal a code
- Final code unlocks the case (prize envelope)

**How to Play:**
1. Groups open Evidence Bag 1, solve questions, derive code
2. Bring code to Chief Detective (teacher) for verification
3. If correct, get Evidence Bag 2. If wrong, try again.
4. First group to crack all codes "escapes the crime scene"

**Materials:** Envelopes, question sets, codes, prize envelope

---

### 3. Whodunit? Speed Round
**Type:** Individual competitive | **Time:** 15 min | **Best for:** Vocabulary, facts, computation

**How to Play:**
- Project questions one at a time with 10-second timer
- Students write answers on whiteboards
- Correct = 1 point. Wrong = 0.
- Every 5 questions: a "Whodunit?" bonus round — teacher reads a riddle/brain teaser worth 3 points
- Highest score = "Sherlock of the Day"

**Materials:** Projected questions, whiteboards, timer

## 👤 INDIVIDUAL ACTIVITIES

### 4. Detective Notebook (Daily Journal)
**Type:** Individual | **Time:** 10 min daily | **Best for:** Reflection, writing

**Instructions:**
Students write daily entries in their Detective Notebook:
- **Monday:** "Evidence collected today: [what I learned]. Primary suspect (hardest topic): [topic]. Lead to follow: [what to study]."
- **Tuesday:** "Numbers analysis: [math reflection]. The numbers reveal: [insight]."
- **Wednesday:** "Forensic report: [science/SS reflection]. Evidence supports: [key fact]."
- **Thursday:** "Investigation summary: My strongest evidence is [skill]. Weakest lead: [skill]."
- **Friday:** "Closing statement: I'm ready to crack the STAAR because [reason]."

**Materials:** Detective Notebook (stapled pages with cover)

---

### 5. Suspect Profile (Weakness Analysis)
**Type:** Individual | **Time:** 15 min | **Best for:** Metacognition

**Instructions:**
Students create a "Suspect Profile" for their hardest test topic:
- **Suspect Name:** (e.g., "Fractions" or "Author's Purpose")
- **Last Seen:** (when did this topic give you trouble?)
- **Known Tactics:** (what makes this topic confusing?)
- **How to Apprehend:** (specific strategies to master it)
- Sketch of the "suspect" (fun drawing)

Post on a "Most Wanted Wall" in the classroom.

**Materials:** Template or blank paper, markers

---

### 6. Clue Card Collection (Flashcard Study)
**Type:** Individual | **Time:** 15 min to create, 5 min/day to use

**Instructions:**
Students create "Clue Cards" — flashcards styled as evidence:
- Front: "EVIDENCE #[number]" — term or concept
- Back: "FINDINGS:" — definition, example, memory trick
- Cards stored in an "Evidence Bag" (ziplock or envelope)
- Daily: 5 minutes of self-quizzing with clue cards

**Materials:** Index cards, markers, ziplock bags/envelopes

## 👥 PARTNER ACTIVITIES

### 7. Good Cop / Bad Cop (Partner Problem Solving)
**Type:** Partner | **Time:** 20 min | **Best for:** Math, reading comprehension

**Instructions:**
- Good Cop = solves carefully, shows all work, explains thinking
- Bad Cop = challenges every step: "Prove it!" "Are you sure?" "Check that!"
- Switch roles every 3 problems
- Both must sign off on every answer

**Materials:** Problem set, paper, pencils

---

### 8. Witness Interview (Partner Quiz)
**Type:** Partner | **Time:** 15-20 min | **Best for:** Any subject

**Instructions:**
Each student creates 5 "interview questions" (review questions) about the day's topic. Then they "interview" their partner:
- Ask the question
- Partner answers
- If correct: "Witness is reliable!" ✓
- If incorrect: "Witness needs more evidence." Review together.
- Switch roles

**Materials:** Paper, pencils

---

## 👥👥 GROUP ACTIVITIES

### 9. Crime Lab Stations (Rotation)
**Type:** Groups of 4-5 | **Time:** 30 min | **Best for:** Multi-topic review

**Stations:**
- 🔬 **DNA Lab** — Reading comprehension (analyzing the "genetic code" of a passage)
- 🔢 **Numbers Lab** — Math computation and word problems
- 🧪 **Forensics Lab** — Science questions
- 📁 **Cold Case Files** — Social studies / mixed review

Groups rotate every 7 minutes. Record findings on "Lab Report" sheet.

**Materials:** Station question sets, lab report sheets, timer

---

### 10. Case Board Assembly (Collaborative Study Guide)
**Type:** Groups of 3-4 | **Time:** 25 min | **Best for:** End-of-week synthesis

**Instructions:**
Each group creates a "case board" (poster) for one topic — like a detective's evidence wall:
- Key terms connected with string/lines (drawn)
- "Evidence" (examples and facts) posted as sticky notes or written cards
- "Red herrings" identified (common wrong answers/mistakes)
- "Conclusion" (summary of the topic)

Post all case boards. Students do a "gallery walk" to review.

**Materials:** Poster board or butcher paper, markers, sticky notes
