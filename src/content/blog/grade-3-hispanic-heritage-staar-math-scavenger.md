That is fine for a quiet Friday. It is not the teaching problem Grade 3 Texas teachers are actually trying to solve between **September 15 and October 15**.

The real ask: celebrate Hispanic and Latino community and contributions **while kids still practice STAAR Math** — one-step and two-step problems within 1,000, multiplication facts through 10×10, and number-pair tables — without turning cultural month into a coloring book with a Spanish title sticker.

If your kids can finish a color-by-code and still freeze on a festival word problem or a neighborhood-map table on Thursday’s CFA, you already know the gap.

## The TEKS that matter in a September math block

Talk about them the way you plan, not as a standards dump.

**TEKS 3.4A** is fluent one-step and two-step addition and subtraction within 1,000 using place value, properties, and the add/subtract relationship. In the room that means: can they read a mercado or festival story, choose the operation, and finish without you tapping the key words?

**TEKS 3.4F** is automaticity on facts through 10×10 and the matching division facts. Heritage Month is a terrible week to park fact practice. Fold it into the theme so it still feels like celebration, not a flash-card funeral.

**TEKS 3.5E** is representing real-world relationships with number pairs in a table and verbal descriptions. That is the STAAR item shape teachers keep underestimating — kids who “get” the story still misread the table columns.

Those three travel together in a scavenger format because stations can specialize, then a rally mixes them the way a STAAR set does. One worksheet maze cannot carry that arc.

## Classroom pitfalls that look like “we did Hispanic Heritage”

**Color-by-code as the whole month.** Kids enjoy it. They do not practice 3.4A story structure, 3.4F automaticity under mild pressure, or 3.5E table reading. Save it for early finisher — do not let it eat your math block.

**Culture as costume, math as afterthought.** Stereotypes, caricature costumes, or licensed characters do not belong in a Texas Grade 3 classroom pack. Celebrate community, family, markets, festivals, and contributions. Keep the math real.

**Escape-room vibes with bare facts.** A lock icon next to `8 + 6` is not STAAR practice. You want stations with word problems, multi-select energy, and a recovery path when kids miss — not a shame spiral that makes you the answer key.

**Skipping the table station.** Addition and facts feel busy. Number-pair tables are where STAAR quietly separates kids who practiced from kids who only colored. If September never touches 3.5E, October CFA will feel like a different sport.

## What a good Heritage Month math scavenger looks like

Keep setup light enough for a 45–60 minute block or a two-day station split.

1. **Present mode or posted stations.** Four hunt stops, then a short rally. No scavenger chaos required if you stay digital.
2. **Pairs with a talk job.** “What is the table asking? What stays the same in each row?”
3. **Chips or letters that build a short code.** Correct work earns progress. Wrong answers loop with a hint, not a lecture from you at every desk.
4. **Self-checking slides plus a paper tracker.** Pencil work keeps kids honest about the operation they chose and the table they read.
5. **A mixed rally at the end.** 3.4A, 3.4F, and 3.5E in one finish — the closest thing to how STAAR stacks skills.

Mercado walk → festival facts → family kitchen two-steps → neighborhood map tables → heritage rally is a clean September arc.
