# Part 1: Domain-by-Domain Breakdown with Distinguished Indicators

---

## Domain 1: Planning

### What Appraisers Look For

Planning is evaluated based on your lesson plans, unit plans, and the evidence that you designed instruction intentionally.

### The Scoring Continuum

| Level | What It Looks Like |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Improvement Needed** | Plans are incomplete, lack alignment to standards, or show no differentiation |
| **Developing** | Plans align to standards but are generic; limited differentiation |
| **Proficient** | Plans are standards-aligned, include clear objectives, and address diverse learners |
| **Accomplished** | Plans reflect deep understanding of standards, data-driven adjustments, and strong differentiation |
| **Distinguished** | Plans are exemplary — student-centered, data-driven, cross-curricular, and responsive to individual needs |

### 🌟 Distinguished Indicators — What Sets You Apart

To score Distinguished in Planning, your appraiser needs to see:

- [ ] **Data-driven design:** You can point to specific student data that informed THIS lesson's design
- [ ] **Individualized adjustments:** Plans include specific accommodations by name/group (not just "ELL students will...")
- [ ] **Cross-curricular connections:** Lessons intentionally connect to other subject areas
- [ ] **Student voice in planning:** Students had input on learning activities, topics, or pace
- [ ] **Vertical alignment:** You reference what students learned before and where they're headed
- [ ] **Anticipation of misconceptions:** Plans document common student errors and how you'll address them
- [ ] **Multiple assessment types:** Plans include formative, summative, and self-assessment opportunities
- [ ] **Real-world relevance:** Clear connection to students' lives, interests, or future applications

### Pro Tips for Domain 1
1. **Annotate your lesson plans** — add margin notes showing WHY you made each choice
2. **Reference data explicitly** — write "Based on Unit 3 results showing 60% struggled with fractions, this lesson..."
3. **Include student names** (in your personal copy) — "Javier and Maria need sentence stems" shows individualization
4. **Show your planning process** — save drafts that show how plans evolved based on data

## Domain 2: Instruction

### What Appraisers Look For

This is the "during the lesson" domain. Appraisers observe how you deliver instruction, engage students, and adjust in real time.

### The Scoring Continuum

| Level | What It Looks Like |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Improvement Needed** | Teacher-centered lecture; students passively receive information |
| **Developing** | Some student engagement but mostly teacher-directed; limited questioning |
| **Proficient** | Good mix of strategies; students are engaged; questioning goes beyond recall |
| **Accomplished** | Student-centered instruction; high-level questioning; real-time adjustments based on student responses |
| **Distinguished** | Students drive the learning; instruction is seamless and responsive; every student is challenged at their level |

### 🌟 Distinguished Indicators

- [ ] **Students lead learning:** Students explain concepts to each other, ask their own higher-order questions
- [ ] **Seamless differentiation:** You adjust complexity, pacing, or support for different groups without stopping the flow
- [ ] **Real-time data use:** You visibly check understanding and modify instruction on the spot
- [ ] **Higher-order thinking is the norm:** Students analyze, evaluate, and create — not just remember
- [ ] **Academic vocabulary is student-owned:** Students use content vocabulary naturally in discussions
- [ ] **Multiple modalities:** Visual, auditory, kinesthetic learning happening simultaneously
- [ ] **Technology enhances (not replaces):** Tech is used to deepen learning, not just for engagement
- [ ] **All students are challenged:** GT students have extensions; struggling students have scaffolds — during the same activity

### Pro Tips for Domain 2
1. **Plan your questions in advance** — write 3-5 higher-order questions on your lesson plan
2. **Use a visible formative assessment** — whiteboards, thumbs up/down, or quick tech poll during the lesson
3. **Have a "pivot plan"** — if 50%+ get it wrong on the mid-lesson check, what will you do?
4. **Let students talk more than you** — aim for 70/30 student talk to teacher talk

## Domain 3: Learning Environment

### What Appraisers Look For

The physical and emotional environment of your classroom. This is evaluated from the moment the appraiser walks in.

### The Scoring Continuum

| Level | What It Looks Like |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Improvement Needed** | Disorganized, off-task behavior, tense atmosphere |
| **Developing** | Basic organization; teacher maintains control but compliance-based |
| **Proficient** | Organized, positive climate; students follow routines; mutual respect evident |
| **Accomplished** | Student-centered environment; students take ownership; strong community feel |
| **Distinguished** | Students self-manage; classroom is a learning community; students hold each other accountable |

### 🌟 Distinguished Indicators

- [ ] **Student self-management:** Students transition, get materials, and start work WITHOUT teacher direction
- [ ] **Peer accountability:** Students respectfully redirect each other when off-task
- [ ] **Student-created environment:** Word walls, displays, or rules were created BY students
- [ ] **Flexible use of space:** Students choose where/how to work based on the task
- [ ] **Visible learning goals:** Students can articulate what they're learning and why
- [ ] **Celebratory culture:** Student achievements and growth are publicly celebrated
- [ ] **Restorative practices:** Conflicts are handled through dialogue, not just consequences
- [ ] **Inclusive environment:** Every student has a visible place and voice in the classroom

### Pro Tips for Domain 3
1. **Data wall** — display class growth data (anonymized) where students can see progress
2. **Student jobs/leadership** — assign meaningful classroom roles that students rotate
3. **Procedure anchor charts** — created WITH students, posted visibly
4. **Flexible seating** — even if you can't buy furniture, offer choice in where students sit/work

## Domain 4: Professional Practices & Responsibilities

### What Appraisers Look For

This domain is evaluated holistically across the year, not just during observations.

### The Scoring Continuum

| Level | What It Looks Like |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Improvement Needed** | Minimal participation in school community; poor communication; no growth efforts |
| **Developing** | Meets basic requirements; attends required PD; communicates when prompted |
| **Proficient** | Active participant; regular parent communication; pursues relevant PD |
| **Accomplished** | Leader among peers; proactive communication; seeks growth opportunities beyond requirements |
| **Distinguished** | Transforms school culture; mentors others; leads professional learning; advocates for students and profession |

### 🌟 Distinguished Indicators

- [ ] **You lead PD:** You've presented to colleagues, led a PLC, or facilitated campus training
- [ ] **Active mentoring:** You formally or informally support other teachers' growth
- [ ] **Proactive parent engagement:** You contact families with POSITIVE news, not just problems
- [ ] **Committee leadership:** You don't just serve on committees — you lead or significantly contribute
- [ ] **Professional advocacy:** You participate in district-level work, policy discussions, or community outreach
- [ ] **Continuous learner:** You pursue professional growth beyond what's required (conferences, certifications, reading)
- [ ] **Student advocacy:** You actively participate in ARD/504/LPAC meetings and advocate for student needs
- [ ] **Reflective practitioner:** You can articulate your growth areas and what you're doing about them

### Pro Tips for Domain 4
1. **Keep a communication log** — every parent email, call, or conference noted with date
2. **Document PLC contributions** — save agendas where YOUR name appears as presenter/contributor
3. **Volunteer for visible roles** — lead a campus initiative, mentor a new teacher, present at a staff meeting
4. **Pursue one meaningful PD** — a book study, conference, or certification that you can discuss authentically

# Part 3: During-Observation Checklist

## What to Have Visible and Ready When Your Appraiser Walks In

### 🏫 Classroom Environment (Check Before They Arrive)

**Physical Space:**
- [ ] Room is clean and organized
- [ ] Student seating is intentional (groups, pairs, or arrangement that matches the lesson)
- [ ] Learning objective is posted and visible (written in student-friendly language)
- [ ] Agenda/schedule is posted
- [ ] Word wall is current and relevant to the unit
- [ ] Anchor charts are visible and student-referenced
- [ ] Student work is displayed (recent, high-quality, diverse students represented)
- [ ] Data wall or growth tracking visual is displayed (class or individual — anonymized)
- [ ] Materials are pre-staged and ready (no fumbling during the lesson)
- [ ] Technology is tested and working

**Documents Ready for Appraiser:**
- [ ] Lesson plan (printed copy for appraiser) with TEKS alignment highlighted
- [ ] Seating chart with student names
- [ ] Any relevant IEP/504 accommodation notes (for appraiser context)
- [ ] Data that informed this lesson (printed or on screen)
- [ ] Previous assessment results showing why this lesson was needed

### 📚 During the Lesson — Mental Checklist

**Instruction:**
- [ ] Start with an engaging hook (not "open your books to page...")
- [ ] State the objective clearly — have students repeat or rephrase it
- [ ] Use multiple modalities (visual, auditory, kinesthetic)
- [ ] Ask higher-order questions (use your pre-planned questions!)
- [ ] Wait time — pause 3-5 seconds after asking a question
- [ ] Call on multiple students, not just volunteers (use equity sticks, cold call)
- [ ] Use academic vocabulary and require students to use it
- [ ] Monitor student work while circulating (don't sit at your desk)
- [ ] Provide specific feedback ("Great job identifying the main idea" not just "Good job")
- [ ] Adjust instruction if students look confused (this shows responsiveness!)

**Student Engagement:**
- [ ] Students are talking more than you
- [ ] Students are working collaboratively at some point
- [ ] Students can explain what they're learning and why
- [ ] Students are using academic language
- [ ] ALL students are engaged (check corners, back row, quiet students)

**Differentiation (Visible):**
- [ ] Different tasks/scaffolds for different levels
- [ ] Sentence stems, graphic organizers, or supports visible for ELL/struggling students
- [ ] Extension activities available for early finishers/GT
- [ ] You interact with students at different levels during independent work

**Assessment:**
- [ ] Formative check during the lesson (whiteboards, thumbs, poll, partner share)
- [ ] Exit ticket or closure activity at the end
- [ ] You reference or collect data from the formative check

### ⏰ Timing

- [ ] Lesson starts on time (bell-to-bell instruction)
- [ ] Transitions are smooth (under 2 minutes)
- [ ] Pacing is appropriate (not rushed, not dragging)
- [ ] Closure happens BEFORE the bell (not cut off by dismissal)
