## Lesson 1: Ecosystem Escape Room 🔓

**Grade:** 5th
**TEKS:** 5.12A, 5.12B
**Duration:** 60 minutes
**Materials:** 5 "Lock Stations" (envelopes with puzzles), ecosystem picture cards, food chain/food web diagrams, "Escape Room Passport," colored pencils, timer

### Learning Objective
*I can describe how energy flows through an ecosystem from producers to consumers to decomposers, and explain how organisms depend on living and nonliving components.*

### STAAR Connection
STAAR Science frequently tests **food chains/webs, energy flow, and ecosystem relationships**.

---

### Warm-Up (8 minutes): "Ecosystem Brainstorm Race"

Teams of 4 fill quadrants: PRODUCERS | CONSUMERS | DECOMPOSERS | NONLIVING THINGS

2-minute timer — brainstorm as many examples as possible.

**Key vocabulary:**
- **Producer:** Makes own food using sunlight
- **Consumer:** Eats other organisms (herbivore, carnivore, omnivore)
- **Decomposer:** Breaks down dead organisms
- **Ecosystem:** All living and nonliving things interacting together

---

### Main Activity (30 minutes): "The Great Ecosystem Escape"

5 stations around the room. Teams solve puzzles to get codes.

**Station 1: "Energy Flow Decoder"**

Arrange organisms into a food chain:
Cards: Hawk, Grasshopper, Sun, Grass, Frog
**Answer: Sun → Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Hawk**

Follow-up: Which is the producer? Primary consumer? Where does ALL energy originate?
**CODE = Number of consumers (3)**

**Station 2: "Food Web Mystery"**

Food web diagram: Grass → Rabbit → Fox; Grass → Mouse → Snake → Hawk; Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake; Dead organisms → Mushrooms

Questions: Name two organisms that eat grass. If mouse population disappeared, what happens to snakes? What role do mushrooms play?
**CODE = Arrows pointing TO the snake (2)**

**Station 3: "Nonliving Factors Challenge"**

Match scenarios to nonliving factors (temperature, water, sunlight, soil/minerals).
**CODE = Scenarios where WATER is the factor (2)**
