> **General information, not legal advice.** This post summarizes TEA guidance, not a new rule. Your district decides when and how it adopts T-TESS 2. Read the official TEA letter linked below and ask your campus leaders if you have questions.

On November 12, 2026, TEA sent school systems a "To the Administrator Addressed" letter titled "T-TESS 2 Update and Implementation Survey." It lays out how the updated Texas Teacher Evaluation and Support System will roll out statewide and asks each school system to complete a short survey by December 10, 2026 ([TEA, T-TESS 2 Update and Implementation Survey](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

Here's the good news up front: this is guidance, not a rule, so there's no single "effective date" to panic about. TEA gave us a timeline, and for most of us nothing changes this school year.

## What TEA announced

T-TESS is the commissioner-recommended teacher appraisal system. It launched in 2015 and TEA says 89% of Texas public school systems use it today ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

After the 2025 revision of the Texas educator standards, TEA says T-TESS had to be updated to stay aligned. Over the past two years, TEA worked with educators, school leaders, ESC partners, and stakeholder groups on the update. TEA says the result, T-TESS 2, aligns appraisal expectations with the updated standards while "incorporating teacher feedback and strengthening consistency across appraisers" ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

## The rollout timeline

TEA lists three years ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)):

- **2026–27:** pilot implementation, planning, and statewide preparation.
- **2027–28:** recommended implementation year for school systems adopting T-TESS 2.
- **2028–29:** full statewide transition to T-TESS 2.

Read that wording carefully. TEA says that, "depending on local context, school systems are encouraged to plan for implementation in the 2027–28 school year." That is a recommendation, not a mandate for next year ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

This year, 15 school systems are piloting T-TESS 2. The pilot is checking whether the updated rubric, training, and resources clearly define effective teaching, work in practice, support consistent calibration, and align with school system priorities. TEA reports that 96% of pilot appraisers and 86% of pilot teachers agree the rubric language is clear and understandable. One teacher said, "T-TESS 2 is well organized and easier to understand than before" ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

## What's in the pilot rubric

TEA has posted the T-TESS 2 Pilot Rubric on the T-TESS website, and says it "continues to be refined based on feedback and learnings from pilot districts" ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)). So treat it as a sneak peek, not the final version.

The pilot rubric is organized into four domains: Instructional Preparation, Instructional Delivery, Learning Environment, and Professional Practices and Responsibilities. Each domain groups target skills (indicators) into dimensions, each with a guiding question. Indicators move from Level 1: Improvement Needed through Developing, Proficient, and Accomplished to Level 5: Distinguished, and many levels shift from what the teacher does to what students do ([T-TESS 2 Pilot Rubric](https://teachfortexas.org/Resource_Files/Evaluation_Process/T-TESS_2_Pilot_Rubric.pdf)).

Three details jumped out at me as worth knowing now ([T-TESS 2 Pilot Rubric](https://teachfortexas.org/Resource_Files/Evaluation_Process/T-TESS_2_Pilot_Rubric.pdf)):

- **The 80% rule.** Unless noted, a performance level counts as met only if the teacher or students meet the descriptor "at least 80% of the time during the observation or summative period." When it isn't realistic to see every student, a representative sample of the class must meet it.
- **HQIM matters for planning.** Dimension D1.A, Lesson Design, is marked "only applicable for teachers not using SBOE-approved HQIM materials."
- **Internalization applies to everyone.** D1.B, Instructional Internalization, and D1.C, Supporting All Learners, are marked "applicable for all teachers." At Proficient, D1.B describes preparing by reviewing and pacing lesson components, completing tasks and assessments, and selecting embedded supports with district-approved internalization tools.

## What changes for appraisers

If you're the one doing the observing, TEA expects a shorter path to appraiser certification. Today, appraisers complete three-day AEL training, three-day T-TESS appraiser training, and the certification exam, a total of six training days plus the exam. The proposed T-TESS 2 path is a 3–4 hour asynchronous prerequisite course, a required two-day in-person training, and the T-TESS 2 certification exam. TEA says the model "may be adjusted prior to implementation" ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

TEA also says T-TESS 2 certification for administrators and appraisers who are already certified in T-TESS will be available "at no cost" to school systems during the statewide rollout ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

## What this means in your classroom this year

If your school system isn't one of the 15 pilots, you're still on current T-TESS in 2026–27 ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)). So our [T-TESS observation tips guide](/blog/ttess-observation-tips-tia) still applies this year. If you want ready-made pre- and post-observation planning pages, our [T-TESS Observation Prep Kit](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/T-TESS-Observation-Prep-Kit-Score-Distinguished-PrePost-Observation-Templat-15593390) is built for the current T-TESS cycle.

That said, reading the pilot rubric now still helps. Its focus on internalizing district materials and on what students are doing is worth knowing before your district decides ([T-TESS 2 Pilot Rubric](https://teachfortexas.org/Resource_Files/Evaluation_Process/T-TESS_2_Pilot_Rubric.pdf)).

## What to do now

1. **Ask your principal about 2027–28.** School systems are asked to complete the 5-minute T-TESS 2 Implementation Survey by December 10, 2026. TEA says a response "does not serve as a final commitment" ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).
2. **Skim the pilot rubric**, starting with Domain 1 and the 80% rule ([T-TESS 2 Pilot Rubric](https://teachfortexas.org/Resource_Files/Evaluation_Process/T-TESS_2_Pilot_Rubric.pdf)).
3. **If you're an appraiser or instructional coach,** watch for training news from your ESC. TEA is also offering optional virtual information sessions for school system leaders ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

## Key dates

- **November 12, 2026:** TEA letter on T-TESS 2 ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).
- **December 9, 2026; January 13, February 10, and March 3, 2027:** TEA virtual information sessions, 9:00–9:45 a.m. ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).
- **December 10, 2026:** school system survey due ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).
- **2027–28:** recommended implementation year; **2028–29:** full statewide transition ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is T-TESS 2?

It is the updated Texas Teacher Evaluation and Support System. TEA says it aligns appraisal with the 2025 revision of the Texas educator standards and incorporates teacher feedback ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

### When will my district switch to T-TESS 2?

TEA recommends 2027–28 for school systems adopting T-TESS 2, with a full statewide transition in 2028–29. TEA says the 2027–28 start depends on local context ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

### Does T-TESS 2 change my evaluation this year?

Only if your school system is one of the 15 piloting T-TESS 2 in 2026–27. Otherwise, you stay on current T-TESS this year ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

### What changes for T-TESS appraisers?

TEA anticipates a 3–4 hour asynchronous course, a two-day in-person training, and a certification exam, replacing six training days plus an exam. The model may be adjusted. Certification for currently certified T-TESS appraisers will be at no cost to school systems during rollout ([TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).

### Where can I see the T-TESS 2 rubric?

TEA's T-TESS website posts the T-TESS 2 Pilot Rubric. It is still being refined based on pilot feedback ([T-TESS 2 Pilot Rubric](https://teachfortexas.org/Resource_Files/Evaluation_Process/T-TESS_2_Pilot_Rubric.pdf), [TEA letter](https://tea.texas.gov/taa-letters/t-tess-2-update-and-implementation-survey)).
