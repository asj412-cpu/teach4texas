## Wednesday 1 — Reading Mini-Passage

### Passage: "The New Student"
Maya walked into Room 204 and froze. Twenty pairs of eyes stared at her. She clutched her backpack straps so hard her knuckles turned white. Mrs. Chen smiled warmly. "Class, this is Maya. She just moved here from Austin." A girl in the front row scooted her desk to make room. "You can sit by me," she whispered. "I'm Sofia." Maya felt her shoulders relax for the first time all morning. Maybe this wouldn't be so bad after all.

### Questions:

**1.** How does Maya feel at the BEGINNING of the passage?
A) excited and happy
B) bored and sleepy
C) **nervous and scared**
D) angry and upset

**2.** Which sentence from the passage BEST shows that Maya was nervous?
A) "Maya walked into Room 204 and froze."
B) "Mrs. Chen smiled warmly."
C) **"She clutched her backpack straps so hard her knuckles turned white."**
D) "'You can sit by me,' she whispered."

**3.** Write 2-3 sentences: How do you think Maya's day will go AFTER this moment? Use evidence from the passage to support your answer.

**Sample:** I think Maya will have a good day because Sofia was kind to her and made room. The passage says "Maya felt her shoulders relax," which shows she's starting to feel better. Sofia might become her friend.

## Wednesday 2 — Reading Mini-Passage

### Passage: "The World's Smallest Frog"
Did you know the world's smallest frog is only about the size of a housefly? Scientists discovered the Paedophryne amauensis frog in Papua New Guinea in 2009. At just 7.7 millimeters long, it's also the world's smallest vertebrate — that means the smallest animal with a backbone! These tiny frogs live on the forest floor among dead leaves. Their brown color helps them hide from predators. They don't even go through a tadpole stage like most frogs. Instead, tiny baby frogs hatch directly from eggs. Scientists think there might be even SMALLER frogs out there waiting to be discovered.

### Questions:

**1.** What is the MAIN IDEA of this passage?

B) **Scientists found the world's smallest frog, which is very unique**
C) Frogs are interesting animals
D) Some frogs don't have a tadpole stage

**2.** The word "vertebrate" means —
A) a type of frog
B) **an animal with a backbone**
C) a very small creature
D) an animal that lives on the ground

**3.** Write 2-3 sentences: Name TWO ways this tiny frog is different from most other frogs. Use information from the passage.

## Wednesday 3 — Reading Mini-Passage

### Passage: "Abuela's Garden"
Every Saturday morning, Elena helps her abuela in the garden. They don't just plant flowers — they plant memories. "This rosemary is from your great-grandmother's garden in Mexico," Abuela says, pressing a small green sprig into the soil. "And these marigolds? We plant them for Día de los Muertos to guide our loved ones home." Elena carefully waters each plant, imagining the long journey from Mexico to their little garden in Texas. When a monarch butterfly lands on the marigolds, Abuela smiles. "See? They travel from Mexico too. They always find their way back."

### Questions:

**1.** What is the THEME of this passage?
A) Gardening is hard work
B) **Family traditions connect us to our roots**
C) Butterflies are amazing creatures
D) Saturday mornings are the best

**2.** Why does the author include the detail about the monarch butterfly?
A) To teach about insects
B) To show that Abuela likes butterflies
C) **To connect to the idea of things traveling from Mexico to Texas, like the family**
D) To explain Día de los Muertos

**3.** Write 2-3 sentences: How does the garden help Elena connect to her family's history? Use evidence from the passage.

## Wednesday 4 — Reading Mini-Passage

### Passage: "How Do Birds Know Where to Go?"
Every fall, millions of birds fly south for the winter. Some travel thousands of miles! But how do they know where to go without a map or GPS? Scientists have discovered that birds use several tools to navigate. First, they use the sun and stars to figure out direction — like a built-in compass. Second, some birds can actually sense Earth's magnetic field through tiny particles in their beaks. Third, experienced birds memorize landmarks like rivers, mountains, and coastlines. Young birds learn the route by following older birds on their first trip. The Arctic tern holds the record for the longest migration — 44,000 miles round trip from the Arctic to Antarctica and back!

**2.** The author's purpose in this passage is to —
