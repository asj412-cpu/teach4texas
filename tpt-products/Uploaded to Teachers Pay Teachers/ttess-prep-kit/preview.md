## Domain 1: Planning

### The Scoring Continuum

| Level | What It Looks Like |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Improvement Needed** | Plans incomplete, lack alignment, no differentiation |
| **Developing** | Aligned to standards but generic; limited differentiation |
| **Proficient** | Standards-aligned, clear objectives, addresses diverse learners |
| **Accomplished** | Deep understanding of standards, data-driven, strong differentiation |
| **Distinguished** | Student-centered, data-driven, cross-curricular, responsive to individual needs |

### 🌟 Distinguished Indicators — What Sets You Apart

- [ ] **Data-driven design:** Specific student data informed THIS lesson
- [ ] **Individualized adjustments:** Specific accommodations by name/group
- [ ] **Cross-curricular connections:** Intentional connections to other subjects
- [ ] **Student voice in planning:** Students had input on activities, topics, or pace
- [ ] **Vertical alignment:** Reference what students learned before and where they're headed
- [ ] **Anticipation of misconceptions:** Common errors documented with solutions
- [ ] **Multiple assessment types:** Formative, summative, and self-assessment
- [ ] **Real-world relevance:** Clear connection to students' lives

### Pro Tips for Domain 1

1. **Annotate your lesson plans** — add margin notes showing WHY you made each choice
2. **Reference data explicitly** — "Based on Unit 3 results showing 60% struggled with fractions..."
3. **Include student names** (personal copy) — shows individualization
4. **Show your planning process** — save drafts showing how plans evolved based on data

## Domain 2: Instruction (Sample)

### 🌟 Distinguished Indicators

- [ ] **Students lead learning** — explain concepts to each other, ask higher-order questions
- [ ] **Seamless differentiation** — adjust complexity without stopping flow
- [ ] **Real-time data use** — check understanding and modify on the spot
- [ ] **Higher-order thinking is the norm** — analyze, evaluate, create
- [ ] **All students challenged** — GT extensions and struggling student scaffolds simultaneously
