## SECTION 1: Reference Posters (5 posters, 1 per type)

### Poster 1: SIMILES 🔴
**What is a Simile?**
A simile compares two DIFFERENT things using **"like"** or **"as."**

**Examples:**
- Her smile was **as bright as** the sun.
- He ran **like** the wind.
- The blanket was **as soft as** a cloud.
- She sings **like** an angel.
- The test was **as easy as** pie.

**Tip:** Look for the words LIKE or AS — that's your simile signal!

---

### Poster 2: METAPHORS 🔵
**What is a Metaphor?**
A metaphor says something **IS** something else (but not literally!). No "like" or "as."

**Examples:**
- Time is **money**.
- She is a **shining star**.
- The classroom was a **zoo**.
- His heart is **made of gold**.
- Life is a **roller coaster**.

**Tip:** If it says something IS something impossible, it's a metaphor!

---

### Poster 3: IDIOMS 🟢
**What is an Idiom?**
An idiom is a phrase that **doesn't mean what it literally says**. You have to know the hidden meaning!

**Examples:**
- "Break a leg!" = Good luck!
- "It's raining cats and dogs" = It's raining very hard
- "Piece of cake" = Very easy
- "Hit the books" = Study hard
- "Under the weather" = Feeling sick

**Tip:** If the sentence would be WEIRD if it were real, it's probably an idiom!

---

### Poster 4: PERSONIFICATION 🟣
**What is Personification?**
Personification gives **human qualities** to animals, objects, or ideas.

**Examples:**
- The wind **whispered** through the trees.
- The sun **smiled** down on us.
- The alarm clock **screamed** at 6 AM.
- The flowers **danced** in the breeze.
- Lightning **raced** across the sky.

**Tip:** Can a non-human thing actually DO that? If not, it's personification!

---

### Poster 5: HYPERBOLE 🟡
**What is Hyperbole?**
Hyperbole is an **extreme exaggeration** that is NOT meant to be taken literally.

**Examples:**
- I've told you a **million** times!
- My backpack weighs a **ton**.
- I'm **so hungry** I could eat a horse.
- She's **the fastest** person in the universe.
- It took **forever** to finish that test.

**Tip:** If it sounds TOO extreme to be true, it's hyperbole!

## SECTION 2: Task Cards (40 cards)

### SIMILE TASK CARDS (Cards 1-8)

**Card 1:** Read the sentence: "The baby's skin was as smooth as silk." What two things are being compared? How are they alike?

**Card 2:** Complete the simile: "The thunder was as loud as ___________." Explain why your comparison works.

**Card 3:** Which sentence contains a simile?
A) The stars twinkled in the sky.
B) The stars were like diamonds scattered on velvet.
C) There were millions of stars.
D) The starry night was beautiful.

**Card 4:** Write your OWN simile about your favorite food. Use "as ___ as ___."

**Card 5:** Read: "Her eyes sparkled like fireworks on the Fourth of July." Draw a picture of what this simile makes you SEE in your mind.

**Card 6:** Fix this simile to make it MORE vivid: "The dog was fast like a car." (Hint: What's even faster or more interesting than a car?)

**Card 7:** Read the passage: "Maria stepped onto the stage. Her legs felt like jelly, and her voice was as quiet as a mouse. But when the music started, she sang like a bird soaring over the mountains." How many similes are in this passage? List them.

**Card 8:** STAAR Format — Read: "The old house creaked in the wind like an old man groaning." What does this simile help the reader understand?
A) The house is very old
B) The sounds the house makes
C) An old man lives there
D) The wind is very strong

### METAPHOR TASK CARDS (Cards 9-16)

**Card 9:** Read: "The world is a stage, and we are merely players." What is being compared? What does this mean?

**Card 10:** Is this a simile or a metaphor? "Her laughter is music to my ears." Explain how you know.

**Card 11:** Write a metaphor about school. Remember — NO "like" or "as"!

**Card 12:** Read: "He has a heart of stone." What does this metaphor tell us about this person?
A) He had heart surgery
B) He is unkind and shows no emotion
C) He likes collecting rocks
D) He is very strong

**Card 13:** Change these similes into metaphors:
- "She is AS brave AS a lion" → "She is ___"
- "His words were LIKE daggers" → "His words were ___"

**Card 14:** Read the poem: "My brother is a tornado / Spinning through the house / Leaving mess behind him / As quiet as a mouse — just kidding!" What is the metaphor? What is the simile?

**Card 15:** Create a metaphor about your teacher. Make it a NICE one! 😄

**Card 16:** STAAR Format — "The detective's mind was a steel trap — nothing escaped it." The metaphor "steel trap" suggests the detective is —
A) dangerous and scary
B) sharp and forgets nothing
C) cold and unfriendly
D) trapped and confused

### IDIOM TASK CARDS (Cards 17-24)

**Card 17:** What does "Don't let the cat out of the bag" REALLY mean?
A) Keep your cat inside
B) Don't reveal a secret
C) Be careful with animals
D) Close the bag tightly

**Card 18:** Draw the LITERAL meaning of "It's raining cats and dogs." Then write what it REALLY means.

**Card 19:** Match each idiom to its meaning:
- "Bite the bullet" → ___
- "Cost an arm and a leg" → ___
- "The ball is in your court" → ___
Choices: Very expensive / Face something difficult bravely / It's your decision now

**Card 20:** Your friend says "I'm feeling under the weather." Write a sentence that means the same thing WITHOUT using an idiom.

**Card 21:** Use the idiom "break the ice" in a sentence that shows you understand its meaning.

**Card 22:** Which sentence uses an idiom?
A) She felt butterflies in her stomach before the test.
B) She saw butterflies in the garden.
C) She drew butterflies on her paper.
D) She read a book about butterflies.

**Card 23:** Create your OWN idiom! Write a phrase that means "to study very hard" but sounds silly if you take it literally.

**Card 24:** STAAR Format — Read: "When the principal walked in, everyone was on their best behavior." The idiom "on their best behavior" means the students were —
A) wearing their best clothes
B) acting as good as possible
C) standing in their best positions
D) using their best supplies

### PERSONIFICATION TASK CARDS (Cards 25-32)

**Card 25:** Read: "The old tree's branches reached out like arms, welcoming anyone who walked by." What human quality is given to the tree?

**Card 26:** Write a sentence giving the MOON a human action or feeling.

**Card 27:** Which sentence uses personification?

## SECTION 4: "Say What You Mean" Board Game

### Setup:
- Print game board (colorful path of 30 spaces, START to FINISH)
- Print and cut 40 game cards (the task cards can double!)
- Each player needs a game piece (eraser, paperclip, etc.)
- One die per group

### Rules:
1. Roll the die, move that many spaces.
2. Draw a figurative language card.
3. Read the sentence aloud.
4. Identify the type (simile, metaphor, idiom, personification, or hyperbole).
5. Explain what it means in your own words.
6. If the group agrees you're correct, stay. If not, go back 2 spaces.
7. **BONUS SPACES** (stars on the board): "Write Your Own!" — create an original example of whatever type the card shows. If the group likes it, move ahead 3 extra spaces.
8. First to FINISH wins!

### Game Board Design:
- Colorful winding path (30 spaces)
- Every 6th space is a BONUS star
- Fun illustrations: books, pencils, speech bubbles
- Color-coded spaces match figurative language types

### Activity 2: Idiom Comic Strip

### Activity 3: "Upgrade My Writing" Challenge
**Instructions:** Take this BORING paragraph and make it AMAZING using figurative language!

**Original:**
"It was a hot day. I walked to school. The sun was bright. I was tired. My backpack was heavy. School seemed far away. When I finally got there, I was happy."

**Challenge:** Rewrite using at least 5 examples of figurative language. Underline each one and label the type in the margin.

**Model answer (for teacher):**
"The sun was an angry fireball (metaphor) scorching everything below. I dragged myself to school, my legs heavy as concrete (simile). My backpack tried to pull me down to the sidewalk (personification) — it weighed a thousand pounds (hyperbole). Would I ever get there? The school was a million miles away (hyperbole). When I finally walked through those doors, I was on cloud nine (idiom)."

## SECTION 7: Assessment (20 Questions)

### Part A: Multiple Choice (15 questions)

4. "I almost died of embarrassment." This is an example of —
A) Simile  B) Metaphor  C) Idiom  D) **Hyperbole**

5. "She has a heart of gold." This is an example of —
A) Simile  B) **Metaphor**  C) Personification  D) Hyperbole

6. "Break a leg!" means —
A) Hurt yourself  B) Run away  C) **Good luck**  D) Be careful

7. "He runs like a cheetah." This is —
A) **Simile**  B) Metaphor  C) Personification  D) Idiom

8. "The flowers danced in the breeze." This is —
A) Simile  B) Metaphor  C) **Personification**  D) Hyperbole

9. "I've read this book a million times." This is —
A) Simile  B) Idiom  C) Personification  D) **Hyperbole**

10. "It's raining cats and dogs" is an example of —
A) Simile  B) Metaphor  C) **Idiom**  D) Personification

11. Which sentence does NOT contain figurative language?
A) The stars winked at me.
B) She ate her dinner quickly.
C) Time is money.
D) He's as tall as a skyscraper.
**Answer: B**

12. "The old car coughed and sputtered to life." The author uses personification to show —
A) The car needs gas  B) **The car is old and struggling**  C) Someone is sick in the car  D) The engine is brand new

13. Read: "Completing that puzzle was a breeze." The idiom "a breeze" means —
A) It was windy  B) **It was very easy**  C) It was refreshing  D) It took a long time

14. Which contains BOTH a simile AND personification?
A) "The river sang as it flowed like silk through the valley."
B) "The river was long and cold."
C) "The river was a ribbon of blue."
D) "I love the river near my house."
**Answer: A** (sang = personification, like silk = simile)

15. "Her eyes were oceans of sadness." This metaphor suggests —
A) Her eyes were blue  B) She was swimming  C) **She was extremely sad**  D) She likes the ocean

### Part B: Short Answer (5 questions)

16. Write your own simile comparing a thunderstorm to something.
**Sample:** "The thunderstorm roared like an angry lion."

17. What does the idiom "hit the nail on the head" mean? Use it in a sentence.
**Sample:** It means to get something exactly right. "When Maria said the answer was 42, she hit the nail on the head."

18. Read: "The trees whispered secrets to each other as the children walked by." What type of figurative language is this? How does it make the scene feel?
**Sample:** Personification — it makes the forest feel alive and mysterious, like the trees are characters.

19. Write a sentence using hyperbole about how much homework you have.
**Sample:** "I have so much homework tonight that I won't finish until I'm 100 years old."

20. Explain the difference between a simile and a metaphor. Give one example of each.
**Sample:** A simile compares using "like" or "as" (Her smile was like sunshine). A metaphor says something IS something else without like/as (Her smile was sunshine). Both compare, but similes use signal words.
