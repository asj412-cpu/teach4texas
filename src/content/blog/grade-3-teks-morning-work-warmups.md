Texas Grade 3 morning work has a quiet failure mode. The tub looks full. Kids are “busy.” You get ten minutes to take attendance, check notes, and start the real block. Then Thursday’s CFA asks a **3.4A** two-step within 1,000 or a **3.11D** edit in context, and the room acts like those skills never showed up before 8:15.

That is what busywork morning work does. It occupies. It does not rehearse the TEKS you are about to teach.

If your packet could be sold in any state with the Texas title sticker swapped on, you already know the gap. Common Core morning work is not a Texas warmup. A coloring sheet with a lone star in the corner is not a TEKS warmup. An escape room is not morning work — it eats the block.

The real ask: **10–15 minutes**, mixed **Math + RLA**, every page labeled with the Texas TEKS you can defend in a PLC, printable when the carts are in someone else’s hallway, digital when you need Easel.

## The TEKS that should be visible before the bell work is done

Talk about them the way you plan, not as a standards dump. A four-week set will rotate, but Week 1 of a serious Grade 3 pack should already look like Texas — not a generic “back to school” packet.

**TEKS 3.2A** is composing and decomposing numbers up to 100,000. In morning work that means: can they see place value without you hovering over the thousands period?

**TEKS 3.4A** is one-step and two-step addition and subtraction within 1,000. If the warmup never asks them to choose the operation in a short story, you are not warming 3.4A. You are filling boxes.

**TEKS 3.4E** is representing multiplication facts through 10×10 — equal groups, arrays, skip counting — and using that picture to find the product. Morning work is a terrible place to park a 50-problem speed sheet and call it STAAR prep. One tight representation, used, beats a wall of isolated products.

**TEKS 3.5E** is number-pair tables and verbal descriptions of real-world relationships. This is the item shape Grade 3 teachers keep underestimating. Kids who “got” the story still misread the columns. If morning work never touches a table, your math block owns the whole recovery.

On the RLA side, the same rule: name the standard on the page.

**TEKS 3.3B** is using context to determine the meaning of unfamiliar words. A vocab box with no sentence is not 3.3B.

**TEKS 3.6G** is evaluating details to determine key ideas. Short text. One job. Not a novel study before announcements.

**TEKS 3.11C** is revising drafts for coherence — the sentence that actually keeps the author’s meaning.

**TEKS 3.11D** is editing drafts for conventions. STAAR does not ask them to circle a noun in isolation at 7:55. It asks them to fix the draft.

Those eight are a sample Week 1 map, not a year-long scope. The point is visibility. If you cannot point to the TEKS code on the student page, it is busywork wearing a Texas hat.

## Classroom pitfalls that look like “we did morning work”

**Unlabeled packets.** Kids finish. You cannot tell a parent, an AP, or your PLC which standard got five minutes of independent reps. If the TEKS are only in your head, the warmup will drift toward whatever is easiest to copy.

**Busywork that feels productive.** Word searches, color-by-code, and “fast finisher” puzzles keep the room quiet. They do not rehearse 3.4A story structure, 3.5E tables, or 3.11D editing in context. Save the quiet craft for after the warmup is done.

**Escape-room energy at 7:50.** Cadet Corps hunts and mysteries have a job — a 45–60 minute block with stations and a rally. That is not the first fifteen minutes. Morning work should be calm, one page, checkable with a key while you take lunch count.

**Print-only or digital-only.** Chromebooks stay on the cart some days. The lab is booked other days. If the set cannot print *and* assign in TPT Easel, you will skip it the week the format does not match your room.

**Common Core in a Texas folder.** If the page cites 3.NBT or “RI.3.1” you are practicing someone else’s test. Texas teachers need Texas codes — **3.2A**, **3.4A**, **3.11D** — on the student paper.

## What a good Grade 3 warmup actually looks like

Keep it light enough that it survives a real Tuesday.

1. **One page. Ten to fifteen minutes.** Mixed Math + RLA so you are not running two packets.
2. **TEKS codes on the student page.** Kids can see the target. You can defend the minutes.
3. **Print for tubs and centers, Easel when you need digital response.** Same items, two delivery modes. No fake “force copy” Google links. No scavenger chaos.
4. **Answer key you can scan from the door.** Morning work that needs a 20-minute debrief is a lesson, not a warmup.
5. **Kid-safe Cadet Corps tone.** Texas and America-first energy — Mia, Jake, Avery starting the day like cadets — without turning 8:00 into a lock-and-code production.

Four days a week, four weeks, is a honest first set: **16 days**. Enough to prove the routine before you buy another month of filler.
