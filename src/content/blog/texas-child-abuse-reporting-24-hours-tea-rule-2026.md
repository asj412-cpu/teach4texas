> **General information, not legal advice.** This post summarizes an adopted state rule. Your district's board-adopted policy controls the details for your campus. Read the official rule text linked below and ask your district if you have questions.

The Texas Education Agency has adopted an amendment to 19 TAC §103.1401 and a new §103.1402. They cover how school employees report suspected child abuse or neglect, including trafficking of a child, and how TEA checks that school systems follow those laws. The rules appeared in the [Texas Register on October 23, 2026](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html) and took **effect October 25, 2026** ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

Two things change for classroom teachers. District reporting policies must now require a report within 24 hours or less (down from 48) under 19 TAC §103.1401(b)(1), and they now have to include safeguards on digital communication between staff and students ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)). Your own duty under Texas Family Code §261.101(b) still applies too: as a professional, you report "not later than the 24th hour" after you first have reasonable cause to believe a child has been or may be abused or neglected, and you "may not delegate to or rely on another person to make the report" ([Texas Family Code §261.101](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/FA/htm/FA.261.htm#261.101)).

## Why the rule changed

TEA says the shorter timeline aligns with SB 571, 89th Texas Legislature, Regular Session, 2025. New §103.1402 implements that same bill by setting procedures for investigating school systems' compliance with child abuse and neglect reporting laws ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

The rules were proposed in the July 10, 2026 Texas Register (51 TexReg 4452). Public comments ran from July 10 to August 10, 2026, and TEA adopted the rules with changes in response to those comments ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

## What teachers must do now

### 1. Report within 24 hours or less

District policies must require every school employee, agent, or contractor who suspects a child's physical or mental health or welfare has been adversely affected by abuse or neglect to make a written or oral report "within 24 hours or less, as determined by the board of trustees, after learning of facts giving rise to the suspicion" ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)). The report goes to at least one of these ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)):

- a law enforcement agency;
- the Texas Department of Family and Protective Services (DFPS), Child Protective Services Division;
- a local Child Protective Services office, where available; or
- the state agency that operates, licenses, certifies, or registers the facility where the alleged abuse or neglect occurred.

Because your board decides the exact window ("24 hours or less"), check your district's updated policy. If the alleged abuse or neglect involves a person responsible for the child's care, custody, or welfare, the policy must require a report to DFPS ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

### 2. You don't have to go through your principal first

District policies may not require school personnel to report suspicions to a school administrator before reporting to one of the agencies above ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)). You can still loop in your campus team if your policy asks you to. It just can't be a required first step.

### 3. Know what your policy must tell you

Policies must notify staff of several things ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)):

- the penalties for failing to report, under Texas Penal Code §39.06, Texas Family Code §261.109, and 19 TAC Chapter 249;
- immunity for people who report or assist an investigation in good faith;
- that oral reports to DFPS are recorded;
- that the person reporting gives their name and phone number, and school employees give their business address and profession;
- that DFPS is not authorized to accept anonymous reports;
- any disciplinary action for not following the district's reporting policy.

### 4. New: transparency in digital communication with students

New §103.1401(b)(9) requires policies to include "safeguards requiring transparency in digital communications between school employees and students designed to prevent improper communication" ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)). The safeguards must prohibit contacting or meeting a student beyond the employee's professional role and making efforts to gain access to or time alone with a student ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

TEA added some room here after public comment. In deciding whether a violation occurred, a district may consider the totality of the circumstances if the contact "arises out of a bona fide, reasonable, and verifiable circumstance" outside the employee's professional role that does not compromise the student's safety and welfare or interfere with parental rights ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)). Commenters, including the Texas School for the Deaf and the Texas Association of School Boards, had raised concerns about family and community relationships with students ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

### 5. Find the policy on your district's website

The policies must be given to all school personnel at the start of each school year and, under the amendment, made available on the local education agency's website. Boards also set how often staff development covers them ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

## What districts must do (§103.1402)

The new section defines "appropriate investigative protocols" for a school system's internal investigations. Those protocols include making required reports and notifications to DFPS, law enforcement, TEA, and the parent or guardian of a student victim (if not otherwise prohibited) within the timeframes the law requires. They also include keeping records TEA can review, cooperating with outside investigations, protecting student safety, and staying objective ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)). The parent or guardian notice was added at adoption ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

TEA may review records, run compliance monitoring, and investigate directly even if no complaint was filed. Based on what it finds, TEA may issue advisory guidance, start a compliance review, issue a formal notice of noncompliance, or require remedial training or corrective action. "Substantial or repeated reporting delays" are listed among the circumstances that may justify escalation ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

## Quick checklist for your classroom

1. Pull up your district's updated reporting policy and note your board's exact window, which will be 24 hours or less ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).
2. Report directly to an approved agency; don't wait on an administrator ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).
3. Check how you text, message, and use social media with students against your district's new digital-communication safeguards ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).
4. Know where your campus's abuse hotline poster hangs. The rule requires one in English and Spanish, with the DFPS Abuse Hotline number and directions to www.txabusehotline.org ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).
5. In an emergency, call 911 ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do Texas teachers now have 24 hours to report suspected child abuse?

Yes. Effective October 25, 2026, district policies must require a report within 24 hours or less, as determined by the board of trustees, after learning of facts giving rise to the suspicion. The previous rule allowed 48 hours ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

### Do I have to tell my principal before I report?

No. District policies may not require school personnel to report to a school administrator before reporting to law enforcement, DFPS, or another listed agency ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

### Can I report anonymously?

No. Policies must tell staff that DFPS is not authorized to accept anonymous reports, and school employees give their name, phone number, business address, and profession ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

### Can I still text or message students?

The rule doesn't ban digital communication. It requires district safeguards for transparency and prohibits contact beyond your professional role. Check your district's updated policy for what is allowed ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).

### Where do I find my district's policy?

The rule requires districts to give the policy to all staff at the start of each school year and make it available on the district or charter website ([Texas Register](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/October232026/Adopted%20Rules/19.EDUCATION.html)).
