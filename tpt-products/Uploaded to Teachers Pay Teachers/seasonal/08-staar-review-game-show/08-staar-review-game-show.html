Summer STAAR Review Game Show — Full Content

Visual Design Notes

  • Color Palette: Electric blue, neon purple, hot pink, gold, black backgrounds (game show aesthetic)
  • Style: TV game show — spotlights, stage curtains, neon signs, confetti animations
  • Layout: Classic Jeopardy grid on main board, individual question slides with dramatic reveals
  • Fonts: Bold block letters for categories, clean readable font for questions
  • Animations: Click to reveal answers, sound effect cues noted in teacher guide

Google Slides Structure

Main Board Slide

  • 10 columns (5 Math + 5 RLA categories)
  • 5 rows (100-500 points)
  • Each cell is a clickable link to the question slide
  • Answered questions can be “X’d out” by the teacher

Question Slide Template

  • Top: Category name + point value
  • Middle: Question in large, readable text
  • Bottom: “Click to reveal answer” button (linked to answer slide)
  • Answer slide shows correct answer + TEKS standard

Navigation

  • Every question slide has a “Back to Board” button
  • Daily Double slides have special animation
  • Final Round slide has wager area

GRADE 3 GAME BOARD

MATH CATEGORY 1: Number Operations (3.4)

100: What is 347 + 258? Answer: 605 (TEKS 3.4A)

200: Sam has 524 stickers. He gives away 189. How many does he have left? Answer: 335 (TEKS 3.4A)

300: What is 7 × 6? Answer: 42 (TEKS 3.4D)

400: There are 8 bags with 9 marbles in each bag. How many marbles are there in all? Answer: 72 (TEKS 3.4D)

500: Maria has 3 boxes of crayons. Each box has 24 crayons. She gives 15 crayons to her friend. How many crayons does Maria have now? Answer: 72 - 15 = 57 (TEKS 3.4A, 3.4K)

MATH CATEGORY 2: Fractions (3.3)

100: What fraction of this shape is shaded? [Circle divided into 4 equal parts, 1 shaded] Answer: 1/4 (TEKS 3.3A)

200: Which is greater: 1/3 or 1/6? Answer: 1/3 (TEKS 3.3H)

300: Name a fraction equivalent to 1/2 with a denominator of 6. Answer: 3/6 (TEKS 3.3F)

400: Place these fractions on a number line: 0, 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 1. Which fraction is closest to 1? Answer: 3/4 (TEKS 3.3B)

500: James ate 2/8 of a pizza. Sara ate 3/8 of the same pizza. Who ate more? How much pizza was eaten in all? Answer: Sara ate more. 5/8 was eaten in all. (TEKS 3.3H, 3.3E)

MATH CATEGORY 3: Geometry & Measurement (3.6, 3.7)

100: How many sides does a hexagon have? Answer: 6 (TEKS 3.6A)

200: What is the perimeter of a square with sides of 5 inches? Answer: 20 inches (TEKS 3.7B)

300: Which unit would you use to measure the length of a classroom — inches, feet, or miles? Answer: Feet (TEKS 3.7A)

400: A rectangle has a length of 8 cm and a width of 3 cm. What is its area? Answer: 24 square cm (TEKS 3.6C)

500 (DAILY DOUBLE): The perimeter of a rectangle is 26 feet. The length is 9 feet. What is the width? Answer: 4 feet (TEKS 3.7B)

MATH CATEGORY 4: Data & Graphs (3.8)

100: Look at this pictograph. Each ☀️ = 2 sunny days. If March has ☀️☀️☀️, how many sunny days were in March? Answer: 6 (TEKS 3.8A)

200: What type of graph uses bars to compare data? Answer: Bar graph (TEKS 3.8A)

300: In a bar graph, the tallest bar shows “Chocolate” with 12 votes. “Vanilla” has 8 votes. How many MORE students chose chocolate? Answer: 4 (TEKS 3.8A)

400: A frequency table shows: Red=5, Blue=8, Green=3, Yellow=4. How many students voted in all? Answer: 20 (TEKS 3.8A)

500: Using the data Red=5, Blue=8, Green=3, Yellow=4, create a question that can be answered using this data AND answer it. Answer: Varies — student-generated (TEKS 3.8B)

MATH CATEGORY 5: Word Problems (3.4K, 3.5)

100: Tony has 15 toy cars. He buys 8 more. How many does he have now? Answer: 23 (TEKS 3.4A)

200: A school has 4 classes. Each class has 22 students. How many students are there? Answer: 88 (TEKS 3.4D)

300: Maya earned $5.75 on Monday and $3.25 on Tuesday. How much did she earn in all? Answer: $9.00 (TEKS 3.4A)

400: There are 56 books to share equally among 7 students. How many books does each student get? Answer: 8 (TEKS 3.4H)

500: A farmer plants 6 rows of corn with 9 plants in each row. He also plants 3 rows of beans with 7 plants in each row. How many plants did he plant in all? Answer: 54 + 21 = 75 (TEKS 3.4K, 3.5B)

RLA CATEGORY 1: Reading Comprehension (3.7)

100: What is the MAIN IDEA of a passage? Answer: What the passage is mostly about (TEKS 3.7A)

200: Read: “The dog wagged its tail, jumped up, and licked the boy’s face.” How does the dog feel? Answer: Happy/excited (TEKS 3.6F)

300: What does it mean to SUMMARIZE a passage? Answer: To retell the most important parts in your own words (TEKS 3.7D)

400: Read: “Lily practiced piano every day for a month. At the recital, she played her song perfectly.” What can you INFER about Lily? Answer: Her practice paid off; she is dedicated/hardworking (TEKS 3.6F)

500: What is the difference between a FICTION and NONFICTION text? Give an example of each. Answer: Fiction = made up (stories, fairy tales); Nonfiction = true/factual (biographies, articles) (TEKS 3.8A-B)

RLA CATEGORY 2: Vocabulary in Context (3.2)

100: What does “enormous” mean? Answer: Very big/huge (TEKS 3.2B)

200: Read: “The car was ancient — it was rusted and falling apart.” What does “ancient” mean here? Answer: Very old (TEKS 3.2B)

300: What is a SYNONYM for “happy”? Answer: Joyful, glad, cheerful, etc. (TEKS 3.2A)

400: What does the PREFIX “un-” mean in “unhappy”? Answer: Not (unhappy = not happy) (TEKS 3.2C)

500 (DAILY DOUBLE): Read: “The scientist was perplexed by the strange results.” What does “perplexed” mean? What CONTEXT CLUES helped you? Answer: Confused/puzzled. “Strange results” is a clue. (TEKS 3.2B)

RLA CATEGORY 3: Author’s Purpose & Text Structure (3.9)

100: What are the 3 main purposes an author writes? (PIE) Answer: Persuade, Inform, Entertain (TEKS 3.9A)

200: An article about how tornadoes form is written to _____. Answer: Inform (TEKS 3.9A)

300: A story about a dragon who saves a village is written to _____. Answer: Entertain (TEKS 3.10A)

400: What text structure is used when an author describes what happened first, next, and last? Answer: Chronological order / sequence (TEKS 3.9D)

500: A letter asking the principal to extend recess is written to _____. Name TWO things the author might include to be convincing. Answer: Persuade. Reasons, facts, examples, emotional appeal, etc. (TEKS 3.9A)

RLA CATEGORY 4: Inference & Text Evidence (3.6F, 3.7C)

100: What does it mean to make an INFERENCE? Answer: To use clues from the text + what you already know to figure something out (TEKS 3.6F)

200: Read: “Marco grabbed his umbrella and raincoat before heading out.” What can you infer about the weather? Answer: It’s raining or about to rain (TEKS 3.6F)

300: What is TEXT EVIDENCE? Answer: Words, phrases, or details directly from the text that support your answer (TEKS 3.7C)

400: Read: “The puppy whimpered and hid under the bed during the fireworks.” How does the puppy feel? What is your evidence? Answer: Scared. Evidence: whimpered, hid under the bed (TEKS 3.6F, 3.7C)

500: Why is it important to use text evidence when answering questions about a passage? Answer: It proves your answer is supported by the text, not just a guess; it makes your answer stronger and more accurate (TEKS 3.7C)

RLA CATEGORY 5: Grammar & Writing (3.11)

100: Is this a COMPLETE sentence? “Running through the park.” Answer: No — it’s a fragment (missing a subject) (TEKS 3.11D)

200: What punctuation goes at the end of a question? Answer: Question mark (?) (TEKS 3.11D)

300: Fix this sentence: “me and him went to the store” Answer: “He and I went to the store.” (TEKS 3.11D)

400: Combine these two sentences using “because”: “Sarah was tired. She stayed up late.” Answer: “Sarah was tired because she stayed up late.” (TEKS 3.11C)

500: Write a sentence using a SIMILE (comparison using “like” or “as”). Answer: Varies — e.g., “The snow was as white as cotton.” (TEKS 3.10D)

Teacher Game Guide

Setup (5 min)

  1. Open Google Slides in presentation mode
  2. Divide class into 2-4 teams
  3. Distribute answer recording sheets
  4. Explain rules

Rules

  • Teams take turns choosing a category and point value
  • All teams can attempt to answer (write on whiteboards or recording sheets)
  • Choosing team answers first; if wrong, other teams can “steal”
  • Daily Doubles: Only the choosing team can answer; they wager points
  • Final Round: All teams wager and answer

Tips

  • Use a 30-second timer for each question
  • Allow discussion within teams (builds collaboration)
  • Play music during thinking time for energy
  • Award bonus points for showing work on word problems
  • Can split into 2 days (Math one day, RLA another)
