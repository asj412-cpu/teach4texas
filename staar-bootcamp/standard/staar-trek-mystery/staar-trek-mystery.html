🔍 STAAR Trek: Mission to Mastery — Quick-Start Overview

Welcome, Detective!

You’ve downloaded STAAR Trek: Mission to Mastery — a detective/mystery-themed STAAR prep week where students solve a week-long case. Each day reveals a new clue, and by Friday, they’ve cracked the case and mastered their STAAR skills.

🔎 The Mystery (Week-Long Arc)

Create a simple mystery that unfolds over the week. Example:

“The Case of the Missing STAAR Scores” Someone has stolen the class’s future STAAR scores! Each day, students uncover a clue by mastering skills. On Friday, they assemble all 5 clues to reveal the “thief” — it was DOUBT all along. The solution? Confidence and preparation!

Reveal one clue each day (written on a card, posted on the “Evidence Board”). By Friday, students piece them together.

🔍 The Week-Long Mystery

“The Case of the Missing STAAR Scores”

Setup: On Monday, announce that “someone” has stolen the class’s confidence/STAAR readiness. Each day, students earn a clue by completing their work. Friday, they assemble all 5 clues.

Clue Cards (prepare in advance): 1. Monday: “The thief strikes when students don’t READ carefully.” 2. Tuesday: “The thief hides in SKIPPED STEPS and rushed calculations.” 3. Wednesday: “The thief feeds on GAPS IN KNOWLEDGE.” 4. Thursday: “The thief is strongest when students PANIC under pressure.” 5. Friday: “The thief’s name is… DOUBT! And YOU have defeated it with PREPARATION!”

Post each clue on the “Evidence Board” at the end of each day.

🔍 MONDAY — Clue #1: The Missing Main Idea

Theme: “A good detective reads everything carefully. Today we investigate texts like crime scenes — every detail matters.”

Case Briefing (10 min)

“Detectives! I’m Chief Detective [Name], and we have a case. Someone — or something — is threatening our STAAR success. This week, we investigate. We gather clues. And on Friday, we crack the case. But we need SHARP minds. Today’s investigation: reading. A detective who can’t read carefully can’t solve anything. Every passage is a crime scene. Every question is a lead. Let’s investigate!”

  • Hand out Detective Badges and Detective Notebooks
  • Show the Evidence Board (empty — “We need clues!”)
  • Explain: complete today’s work = earn Clue #1

Investigation 1 (45 min)

  1. (10 min) “Witness Statements” Warm-Up — 5 quick reading questions. “These witnesses saw something. What can we learn from their statements?”
  2. (20 min) “Crime Scene Analysis” — Teacher models close reading of a STAAR passage. “A detective examines every piece of evidence. Watch how I interrogate this text…” Demonstrate annotation as “evidence collection.”
  3. (15 min) “Partner Investigation” — Pairs work a passage together, taking turns being “lead detective” and “partner.” Must cite evidence for every answer.

Coffee Break (5 min)

  • “Suspect Lineup” — Teacher shows 5 vocabulary words. Students must “identify the suspect” — which word matches the definition given? Quick, active, fun.

Investigation 2 (40 min)

“Case File #1” — Reading Packet - STAAR-format questions framed as “evidence” - “Examine the evidence carefully. Your answers will bring us closer to solving the case.” - Early finishers: “Cold Case” bonus challenge

Differentiation: - Struggling: “Junior Detective” version — guided evidence markers, simpler language - Advanced: “Senior Detective” — complex texts, analytical questions - ELL: Visual evidence cards, bilingual vocabulary

Case Notes (10 min)

  • Review tough questions: “The evidence was tricky here, but look…”
  • Detective’s Rule #1: Post on Evidence Board: “Always find evidence in the text before making an accusation (choosing an answer).”
  • Students who completed the case file earn Clue #1 — post it on the Evidence Board!
  • Detective notebooks: students record today’s key learning

🔍 TUESDAY — Clue #2: The Numbers Don’t Add Up

Theme: “Something doesn’t add up — literally. Today we crunch the numbers and find the mathematical truth.”

Case Briefing (10 min)

“Detectives, Clue #1 told us the thief strikes when we don’t read carefully. Today we follow the money — the NUMBERS. In every great investigation, the numbers tell the story. If the numbers don’t add up, you’ve found a lie. Today, we make sure OUR numbers add up perfectly.”

Investigation 1 (45 min)

  1. (10 min) “Number Interrogation” — Timed computation warm-up. “Interrogate these numbers! Get answers fast!”
  2. (20 min) “Following the Paper Trail” — Teacher models multi-step word problems as “following the money trail.” Show every step. “If we skip a step, we lose the trail!”
  3. (15 min) “Good Cop/Bad Cop” — Partners alternate: one solves (good cop — careful, methodical), one challenges (bad cop — “Are you SURE? Check that step!”). Switch roles.

Coffee Break (5 min)

  • “True or False Interrogation” — 5 math statements, students stand for true, sit for false.

Investigation 2 (40 min)

“Case File #2” — Math Packet - STAAR-format math questions - “The numbers in this case don’t add up. Fix them.”

Case Notes (10 min)

  • Detective’s Rule #2: “Show ALL your work. Evidence that can’t be seen can’t be used.”
  • Earn Clue #2 — add to Evidence Board
  • Update detective notebooks

🔍 WEDNESDAY — Clue #3: The Scientific Evidence

Theme: “A great detective uses science and knowledge of the world. Today we examine the scientific and historical evidence.”

Case Briefing (10 min)

“Two clues down, three to go. Today we examine the SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE. The best detectives in the world use forensic science. They know history. They understand how the world works. Today, science and social studies are our forensic tools.”

Investigation 1 (45 min)

  1. (10 min) “Forensic Lab” Warm-Up — 5 science/SS questions. “Quick forensic analysis!”
  2. (20 min) “Evidence Stations” — 4 stations, each a different “lab”:
    • Forensic Lab A: Science concepts
    • Forensic Lab B: Social studies facts
    • Forensic Lab C: Vocabulary analysis
    • Forensic Lab D: Mixed mystery questions Groups rotate every 5 min.
  3. (15 min) “Suspect Profile” — Students create a “profile” of their weakest topic: what it is, why it’s hard, how to approach it. “Know your suspect!”

Coffee Break (5 min)

  • “Whodunit?” — Teacher reads a short, silly mystery. Students guess the answer. Pure fun.

Investigation 2 (40 min)

“Case File #3” — Science/SS Packet (shorter — 10-12 questions) - Remaining time: “Interrogation Room” Review Game — Teacher vs. Class. Teacher asks increasingly hard questions. Class works together to answer. If they stump the teacher (ask a question teacher “can’t” answer), bonus points!

Case Notes (10 min)

  • Detective’s Rule #3: “When you don’t know, eliminate what you DO know is wrong. Narrow the suspects!”
  • Earn Clue #3 — Evidence Board growing!
  • Midweek treat 🎉

🔍 THURSDAY — Clue #4: The Full Investigation

Theme: “Time for a full investigation. Every skill, every clue, all at once.”

Case Briefing (10 min)

“Detectives, we’re close to cracking this case. Today is the full investigation — every skill, every subject, just like the real STAAR. Treat this like the real deal. Stay focused. Stay sharp. A sloppy investigation misses the truth.”

Investigation 1 (45 min)

“Full Investigation” — Mini Practice Test - 20-30 mixed STAAR-format questions - Test conditions: silent, independent, timed

Coffee Break (5 min)

  • Quiet stretch.

Investigation 2 (40 min)

  1. (15 min) Review answers — “Let’s review the investigation for errors”
  2. (10 min) Self-assessment: strengths, weak areas
  3. (15 min) Small group “special task forces” — grouped by need

Case Notes (10 min)

  • Detective’s Rule #4: “Stay calm under pressure. A panicked detective makes mistakes.”
  • Earn Clue #4
  • “Tomorrow we crack the case.”

🔍 FRIDAY — Clue #5: Case Closed! 🎉

Theme: “All the clues are in. Time to solve the mystery and celebrate.”

Case Briefing (10 min)

“Detectives. Five days of investigation. Four clues on the board. Today… we get the FINAL CLUE and CRACK THE CASE. But first — a few final tools for your detective kit.”

Investigation 1 (45 min)

  1. (15 min) “Detective’s Toolkit” — Review top 10 test-taking strategies
  2. (15 min) “Cold Case Review” — Quick game reviewing commonly missed question types
  3. (15 min) “The Big Reveal” — Reveal Clue #5! Read all 5 clues together. “The thief was… DOUBT! And you’ve DEFEATED it through preparation and hard work!”

Coffee Break (5 min)

Investigation 2: Case Closed Celebration (40 min)

  1. (10 min) “Closing Statement” — Students write their final case notes / letter to themselves
  2. (10 min) “Case Closed” Certificate Ceremony
  3. (10 min) Students sign each other’s detective notebooks
  4. (10 min) Celebration — music, treats, photos

Final Case Notes

  • Detective’s Rule #5: “The truth was inside you all along. Trust your training.”
  • All 5 clues earned = “Master Detective” status
  • “CASE CLOSED. You’re STAAR ready.” 🔍

Elementary, my dear students. You’ve solved it. 🔍

🔍 STAAR Trek: Mission to Mastery — Themed Activities

10 detective-themed activities with 3 review games.

🎮 REVIEW GAMES

1. Interrogation Room (Whole Class)

Type: Class game | Time: 20 min | Best for: Any subject review

How to Play: - Teacher reads a question. The question is the “suspect.” - Students write their answer on whiteboards — this is their “accusation” - Reveal correct answer. Students who got it right “solved the case” for that round - Track: How many cases did the class solve out of 15? - Dramatic narration: “The suspect claims the answer is B. Detective Johnson says C. Let’s check the evidence… C IS CORRECT! Case solved!”

Materials: Questions, whiteboards, markers

2. Escape the Crime Scene (Escape Room)

Type: Small groups (3-4) | Time: 30 min | Best for: Mixed review

Setup: - 4-5 sealed envelopes (“evidence bags”) per group - Each contains a question set; correct answers reveal a code - Final code unlocks the case (prize envelope)

How to Play: 1. Groups open Evidence Bag 1, solve questions, derive code 2. Bring code to Chief Detective (teacher) for verification 3. If correct, get Evidence Bag 2. If wrong, try again. 4. First group to crack all codes “escapes the crime scene”

Materials: Envelopes, question sets, codes, prize envelope

3. Whodunit? Speed Round

Type: Individual competitive | Time: 15 min | Best for: Vocabulary, facts, computation

How to Play: - Project questions one at a time with 10-second timer - Students write answers on whiteboards - Correct = 1 point. Wrong = 0. - Every 5 questions: a “Whodunit?” bonus round — teacher reads a riddle/brain teaser worth 3 points - Highest score = “Sherlock of the Day”

Materials: Projected questions, whiteboards, timer

👤 INDIVIDUAL ACTIVITIES

4. Detective Notebook (Daily Journal)

Type: Individual | Time: 10 min daily | Best for: Reflection, writing

Instructions: Students write daily entries in their Detective Notebook: - Monday: “Evidence collected today: [what I learned]. Primary suspect (hardest topic): [topic]. Lead to follow: [what to study].” - Tuesday: “Numbers analysis: [math reflection]. The numbers reveal: [insight].” - Wednesday: “Forensic report: [science/SS reflection]. Evidence supports: [key fact].” - Thursday: “Investigation summary: My strongest evidence is [skill]. Weakest lead: [skill].” - Friday: “Closing statement: I’m ready to crack the STAAR because [reason].”

Materials: Detective Notebook (stapled pages with cover)

5. Suspect Profile (Weakness Analysis)

Type: Individual | Time: 15 min | Best for: Metacognition

Instructions: Students create a “Suspect Profile” for their hardest test topic: - Suspect Name: (e.g., “Fractions” or “Author’s Purpose”) - Last Seen: (when did this topic give you trouble?) - Known Tactics: (what makes this topic confusing?) - How to Apprehend: (specific strategies to master it) - Sketch of the “suspect” (fun drawing)

Post on a “Most Wanted Wall” in the classroom.

Materials: Template or blank paper, markers

6. Clue Card Collection (Flashcard Study)

Type: Individual | Time: 15 min to create, 5 min/day to use

Instructions: Students create “Clue Cards” — flashcards styled as evidence: - Front: “EVIDENCE #[number]” — term or concept - Back: “FINDINGS:” — definition, example, memory trick - Cards stored in an “Evidence Bag” (ziplock or envelope) - Daily: 5 minutes of self-quizzing with clue cards

Materials: Index cards, markers, ziplock bags/envelopes

👥 PARTNER ACTIVITIES

7. Good Cop / Bad Cop (Partner Problem Solving)

Type: Partner | Time: 20 min | Best for: Math, reading comprehension

Instructions: - Good Cop = solves carefully, shows all work, explains thinking - Bad Cop = challenges every step: “Prove it!” “Are you sure?” “Check that!” - Switch roles every 3 problems - Both must sign off on every answer

Materials: Problem set, paper, pencils

8. Witness Interview (Partner Quiz)

Type: Partner | Time: 15-20 min | Best for: Any subject

Instructions: Each student creates 5 “interview questions” (review questions) about the day’s topic. Then they “interview” their partner: - Ask the question - Partner answers - If correct: “Witness is reliable!” ✓ - If incorrect: “Witness needs more evidence.” Review together. - Switch roles

Materials: Paper, pencils

👥👥 GROUP ACTIVITIES

9. Crime Lab Stations (Rotation)

Type: Groups of 4-5 | Time: 30 min | Best for: Multi-topic review

Stations: - 🔬 DNA Lab — Reading comprehension (analyzing the “genetic code” of a passage) - 🔢 Numbers Lab — Math computation and word problems - 🧪 Forensics Lab — Science questions - 📁 Cold Case Files — Social studies / mixed review

Groups rotate every 7 minutes. Record findings on “Lab Report” sheet.

Materials: Station question sets, lab report sheets, timer

10. Case Board Assembly (Collaborative Study Guide)

Type: Groups of 3-4 | Time: 25 min | Best for: End-of-week synthesis

Instructions: Each group creates a “case board” (poster) for one topic — like a detective’s evidence wall: - Key terms connected with string/lines (drawn) - “Evidence” (examples and facts) posted as sticky notes or written cards - “Red herrings” identified (common wrong answers/mistakes) - “Conclusion” (summary of the topic)

Post all case boards. Students do a “gallery walk” to review.

Materials: Poster board or butcher paper, markers, sticky notes
