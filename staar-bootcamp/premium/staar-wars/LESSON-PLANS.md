# ⭐ STAAR Wars — 5-Day Lesson Plans

> **Mission Commander's Guide to Boot Camp Week**
> Adapt these plans to your grade level (3rd–8th) and tested subjects. The framework is flexible — plug in your specific TEKS-aligned content.

---

## Daily Schedule Template

| Time Block | Duration | Activity |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| Mission Briefing | 10 min | Themed warm-up, daily overview, motivation |
| Training Block 1 | 45 min | Core instruction / guided review |

## 🌟 MONDAY — Mission 1: Launch Prep

**Theme:** "Before we launch, every system must be checked. Today we verify our foundational knowledge."

**Focus:** Reading/ELA Foundations (or your primary tested subject)

### Mission Briefing (10 min)
**Teacher Script:**
> "Good morning, Cadets! Welcome to STAAR Command. I'm your Mission Commander, and this week, you're part of the most elite training program in the galaxy. Every astronaut trains before a launch — and that's exactly what we're doing. STAAR is our launch day, and by Friday, you'll be fully mission-ready. Today's mission: Launch Prep. We're checking every system — every skill — to make sure we're ready for liftoff. Let's go!"

**Activities:**
- Hand out **Cadet ID badges** (see STUDENT-MATERIALS.md)
- Show the **Launch Sequence Countdown** poster — "5 days to launch!"
- Quick preview of the week's missions

### Training Block 1: Guided Review (45 min)

**Reading/ELA Focus Areas (adapt to your grade):**
- **3rd–5th:** Main idea & supporting details, making inferences, author's purpose, vocabulary in context
- **6th–8th:** Summarizing, analyzing literary elements, author's craft, textual evidence

**Structure:**
1. **(10 min) "Systems Check" Warm-Up** — 5 quick multiple-choice questions projected on board, students answer on whiteboards or scrap paper. Review answers together. Frame each question as a "system" being checked (Navigation System = main idea, Communication System = vocabulary, etc.)

2. **(20 min) "Star Map" Guided Practice** — Teacher models a STAAR-format passage and question set. Think-aloud strategy: "Here's how a trained astronaut reads this passage..." Walk through annotation strategies, elimination techniques, and evidence-finding.

3. **(15 min) "Co-Pilot Practice"** — Students pair up. Each pair gets a short passage with 3-4 questions. Partner A reads aloud, Partner B annotates. Then they answer questions together, discussing their thinking. Switch roles for a second passage.

### Fuel Break (5 min)
- Space-themed brain break: "Asteroid Dodge" — students stand, teacher calls out commands (jump left, duck right, spin) to dodge "asteroids." Quick physical reset.

### Training Block 2: Independent Practice (40 min)

**"Mission 1 Packet"**
- STAAR-format passage(s) with 8-12 multiple choice and/or open-ended questions
- Students work independently — this simulates actual test conditions
- Frame it: "This is your first solo mission. Show me what you've got, Cadets."
- Early finishers: "Bonus Transmission" — an extra challenge passage or writing prompt

**Differentiation:**
- **Struggling students:** Provide a "Mission Support" version with vocabulary pre-taught and shorter passages. Pair with a "Senior Cadet" (stronger reader) if needed.
- **Advanced students:** Include a "Commander's Challenge" — an above-grade-level passage or an analytical writing prompt.
- **ELL students:** Provide bilingual glossary for key terms. Allow use of approved dictionaries per STAAR accommodation guidelines.

### Mission Debrief (10 min)
- Review 2-3 most-missed questions from the packet
- **Key Strategy of the Day:** Write on board in "Space Log" format — e.g., "Space Log Entry 1: Always go back to the text for evidence. Never trust your memory in zero gravity."
- Award **Mission 1 Badges** to students who completed the packet
- Update countdown poster: "4 days to launch!"

### Materials Needed:
- [ ] Cadet ID badges (printed)
- [ ] Launch Sequence Countdown poster
- [ ] Whiteboards or scrap paper + markers
- [ ] Mission 1 passage/question packet (teacher provides — use released STAAR questions or your own)
- [ ] Mission 1 Badges (stickers or stamps on badge card)
- [ ] "Bonus Transmission" extra challenge (1-2 pages)

## 🌟 TUESDAY — Mission 2: Orbit Check

**Theme:** "We've cleared the atmosphere. Now we need to verify our calculations are solid. Math keeps us in orbit."

**Focus:** Mathematics

### Mission Briefing (10 min)
**Teacher Script:**
> "Cadets! Yesterday you crushed Launch Prep. Mission 1: complete. Today we're in orbit, and you know what keeps a spacecraft in orbit? MATH. Precise calculations. Today's mission is Orbit Check — we're making sure our math skills are locked in tight. One wrong calculation and we drift into the void. No pressure! Let's go, Cadets!"

**Activities:**
- Update the **Mission Progress Board** (who earned Mission 1 badges?)
- Play a 2-minute space fact video or show an image from NASA's website as a hook
- Preview today's math focus areas

### Training Block 1: Guided Review (45 min)

**Math Focus Areas (adapt to your grade):**
- **3rd–4th:** Multiplication/division facts, fractions, measurement, word problems
- **5th–6th:** Decimals, ratios, geometry basics, data analysis, expressions
- **7th–8th:** Proportional relationships, equations/inequalities, statistics, financial literacy

**Structure:**
1. **(10 min) "Navigation Calculations" Warm-Up** — 5 computation problems displayed. Students solve on whiteboards. Frame: "If you get this wrong, the ship goes off course! Calculate carefully!" Review strategies for each.

2. **(20 min) "Orbit Plotting" Guided Practice** — Teacher models 3-4 STAAR-format word problems. Emphasize: reading the problem twice, identifying what's being asked, showing work, checking reasonableness. Use space contexts where possible: "The space station orbits Earth every 90 minutes. If the crew has been aboard for 3 days..."

3. **(15 min) "Mission Control Pairs"** — Partners work through a set of 6 problems. One is "Mission Control" (checks work, asks questions), the other is the "Pilot" (solves). Switch roles after 3 problems.

### Fuel Break (5 min)
- "Zero Gravity Freeze Dance" — play space-themed music, students dance. When music stops, freeze in a "floating in space" pose. Silly but effective energy reset.

### Training Block 2: Independent Practice (40 min)

**"Mission 2 Packet"**
- 15-20 STAAR-format math problems covering major reporting categories
- Include a mix: multiple choice, griddable (where applicable), and multi-step problems
- Frame: "Orbit Check simulation. Every calculation matters."

**Differentiation:**
- **Struggling students:** "Mission Support" version with fewer problems, worked examples at the top, and a formula/reference sheet
- **Advanced students:** "Commander's Challenge" — problems requiring multi-step reasoning or real-world application
- **ELL students:** Simplified language in word problems; key math vocabulary glossary

### Mission Debrief (10 min)
- Review 2-3 tricky problems as a class
- **Key Strategy of the Day:** "Space Log Entry 2: Always show your work. In space, we check every calculation twice."
- Award **Mission 2 Badges**
- Countdown: "3 days to launch!"

### Materials Needed:
- [ ] Whiteboards + markers
- [ ] Mission 2 math packet (teacher provides)
- [ ] Formula/reference sheet (for Mission Support version)
- [ ] Mission 2 Badges
- [ ] Calculator (if allowed for your grade level)
- [ ] Space-themed music playlist

## 🌟 WEDNESDAY — Mission 3: Deep Space Exploration

**Theme:** "We've gone deeper into space than ever before. Time to explore the unknown — science and the world around us."

**Focus:** Science & Social Studies (or your secondary tested subjects). If your students only test in ELA & Math, use this day for cross-curricular review or deeper dives into weak areas.

### Mission Briefing (10 min)
**Teacher Script:**
> "Cadets, we are now in DEEP SPACE. The easy part is over. Today's mission takes us into uncharted territory — we're exploring science and the world around us. Every great astronaut is also a scientist and a historian. Today, we explore. Let's see what's out there!"

### Training Block 1: Science Review (45 min)

**Science Focus Areas (adapt to grade):**
- **5th:** Matter & energy, force/motion, Earth & space, organisms & environments
- **8th:** Force/motion/energy, matter, Earth & space, organisms/environments, reporting categories

**Structure:**
1. **(10 min) "Scan the Galaxy" Quick Review** — 5 science questions, rapid-fire on whiteboards
2. **(20 min) "Alien Specimens" Lab-Style Review** — Set up 4-5 stations around the room, each with a different science concept. Students rotate in groups (5 min each), answering 2-3 questions per station. Frame: "Each station is a different planet with alien specimens to analyze."
3. **(15 min) "Transmission from Earth" Class Discussion** — Teacher-led review of most commonly tested science concepts. Use a quick sort activity: students categorize vocabulary terms into correct science domains.

### Fuel Break (5 min)
- "Alien Dance-Off" — students create their best alien dance move. Vote on the most creative. Quick, silly, resets energy.

### Training Block 2: Social Studies Review (40 min)

**Social Studies Focus Areas (adapt to grade):**
- **8th:** U.S. History (major eras, key figures, government, geography, economics, citizenship)

**Structure:**
1. **(15 min) "History of the Galaxy" Timeline Activity** — Create a collaborative class timeline of key events. Each student gets a card with an event; they must place themselves in chronological order. Discuss connections.
2. **(25 min) "Mission 3 Packet"** — Mixed science/social studies STAAR-format questions

**Differentiation:**
- **Struggling students:** Concept cards with visuals and simplified definitions
- **Advanced students:** Analysis questions — "Compare and contrast" or "What would have happened if..."
- **ELL students:** Visual-heavy materials, bilingual key terms

### Mission Debrief (10 min)
- **Space Log Entry 3:** "The universe is full of connections. Science explains HOW things work. History explains WHY things happened. Look for patterns."
- Award **Mission 3 Badges**
- Countdown: "2 days to launch!"
- 🎉 **Midweek morale boost:** Hand out small treats or play a quick reward game (see ACTIVITIES.md — "Asteroid Field" review game)

### Materials Needed:
- [ ] Station materials (printed question sets, any manipulatives)
- [ ] Timeline cards (events on index cards)
- [ ] Mission 3 mixed packet
- [ ] Concept vocabulary cards with visuals
- [ ] Midweek treats (optional)
- [ ] Mission 3 Badges

## 🌟 THURSDAY — Mission 4: Re-Entry Simulation

**Theme:** "Re-entry is the most dangerous part of any space mission. It requires focus, precision, and calm under pressure. Today we simulate the real thing."

**Focus:** Full STAAR-Format Mixed Practice (all subjects)

### Mission Briefing (10 min)
**Teacher Script:**
> "Cadets. Listen up. Today is Re-Entry Simulation. This is the closest thing to the real mission you'll experience before launch day. Re-entry is when astronauts have to be most focused — one wrong move and things go sideways. Today, I need your absolute best. You're going to see questions from every subject, just like the real STAAR. Treat this like the real deal. Silence. Focus. You've trained for this."

### Training Block 1: Timed Practice Simulation (45 min)

**"Re-Entry Simulation Exam"**
- Create a mini STAAR-format test (20-30 questions) covering all tested subjects
- Timed — give students a set amount of time (match approximate STAAR pacing)
- Test-like conditions: silent, independent work, no phones, desks separated
- Frame: "This is your re-entry simulation. Mission Control is watching."

### Fuel Break (5 min)
- Quiet break — stretching only. Maintain the serious tone of "simulation day."

### Training Block 2: Review & Strategy (40 min)

**"Post-Simulation Analysis"**
1. **(15 min)** Students self-grade or teacher reviews answers together
2. **(10 min)** Students identify their "weak zones" — which types of questions gave them trouble?
3. **(15 min)** Small group review — group students by weak area. Teacher circulates and provides targeted mini-lessons. Use "Senior Cadets" (students who scored high) as peer tutors.

**Differentiation:**
- Group students by need for the small group portion
- Provide targeted review materials for each group
- Students who aced the simulation become "Mission Specialists" who help others

### Mission Debrief (10 min)
- **Space Log Entry 4:** "Re-entry is hard. But you survived the simulation. The real mission will feel just like this — and now you know what to expect."
- Award **Mission 4 Badges**
- Countdown: "1 day to launch! Tomorrow is our final prep day."
- Motivational moment: Share a story about an astronaut who overcame failure (Mae Jemison, Chris Hadfield, etc.)

### Materials Needed:
- [ ] Mini STAAR-format practice test (20-30 questions, mixed subjects)
- [ ] Answer key / answer document
- [ ] Scrap paper
- [ ] Timer
- [ ] Targeted review materials for small groups
- [ ] Mission 4 Badges

## 🌟 FRIDAY — Mission 5: Countdown to Launch

**Theme:** "Final preparations. Confidence. Calm. You are ready."

**Focus:** Test-Taking Strategies, Confidence Building, Light Review

### Mission Briefing (10 min)
**Teacher Script:**
> "Cadets. This is it. Final day of training. You have worked SO hard this week. You've completed four missions. You've proven you have the skills. Today isn't about cramming — today is about CONFIDENCE. Today we sharpen our strategies, steady our nerves, and prepare for launch. When you walk into that testing room, you will be the most prepared cadets in the galaxy. I believe in you. Let's finish this."

### Training Block 1: Strategy Review (45 min)

**"Mission Commander's Strategy Session"**

1. **(15 min) "Top 10 STAAR Survival Strategies"** — Teacher-led review of the most important test-taking strategies:
   - Read the question FIRST, then the passage (for reading)
   - Eliminate two wrong answers before guessing
   - Show all work in math (even if you can do it in your head)
   - Budget your time — don't spend 10 minutes on one question
   - Go back and check answers if you finish early
   - Trust your first instinct (but verify with evidence)
   - For open-ended responses: restate the question, give evidence, explain
   - Read ALL answer choices before selecting
   - Skip hard questions and come back
   - Stay calm — if you feel stuck, take 3 deep breaths

2. **(15 min) "Error Analysis: Common Traps"** — Show examples of common STAAR trick questions and how to avoid them. Students practice identifying "trap" answer choices.

3. **(15 min) "Rapid Fire Review Game"** — Quick, fun, low-stakes review game (see ACTIVITIES.md — "Warp Speed Challenge"). Focus on high-frequency tested concepts.

### Fuel Break (5 min)
- "Meditation in Space" — 2 minutes of quiet breathing/mindfulness. "Astronauts train their minds, not just their skills."

### Training Block 2: Confidence & Celebration (40 min)

1. **(10 min) "Letters to Myself"** — Students write a short letter to themselves: "What I'm proud of learning this week" and "What I'm going to remember on test day." They seal it in an envelope. Teacher can hand these back before the actual STAAR test.

2. **(10 min) "Mission Complete" Certificate Ceremony** — Call each student up, shake their hand, award their **Mission Complete Certificate**. Make it a moment. Take photos.

3. **(10 min) "Commander's Final Transmission"** — Teacher gives a final pep talk. Read the motivational quote poster aloud. Tell students specifically what you've seen them accomplish this week.

4. **(10 min) Celebration** — Play the space playlist, let students sign each other's "Cadet Yearbook" page (a fun printable where students can write encouraging messages to each other). Hand out small treats if allowed.

### Mission Debrief — Final Entry (10 min)
- **Space Log Entry 5:** "Training complete. All systems go. You are STAAR-ready. Launch when ready."
- All 5 **Mission Badges** earned = full badge card complete
- Countdown: "LAUNCH DAY IS NEXT! 🚀"
- Final cheer: "3... 2... 1... We are STAAR READY!" (students count down together)
