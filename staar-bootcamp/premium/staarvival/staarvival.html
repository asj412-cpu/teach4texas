🌿 STAAR-vival: Escape the Test — Quick-Start Overview

Welcome, Explorer!

You’ve downloaded STAAR-vival — a survival/adventure-themed STAAR prep week where students journey through 5 different terrains, building skills and earning survival badges along the way. By Friday, they’ve “survived” the wilderness and are ready to conquer the STAAR.

  1. Wear khaki or a safari hat. The sillier you go, the more they love it.
  2. Nature sounds are free. YouTube has hours of jungle/ocean/mountain ambiance. Play it softly during independent work.

  4. Tribal Council is the highlight. Save it for the end of each day — it’s the reward.
  5. Survival kits = pencil pouches. Put 2 pencils, an eraser, and a small snack in a ziplock. Label “Survival Kit.” Kids go wild.

Trail Guide’s Handbook — Navigate the week terrain by terrain.

🌴 MONDAY — Jungle Terrain

Theme: “The jungle is dense and full of surprises. Reading is like navigating a jungle — you have to push through the thick text to find what you need.”

Focus: Reading/ELA

Trail Briefing (10 min)

Teacher Script: > “Explorers! Welcome to STAAR-vival. I’m your Lead Explorer, and this week we’re going on the adventure of a lifetime. We’ll cross five terrains — jungle, desert, mountains, ocean, and finally the summit. Each terrain will test different survival skills. Today: the JUNGLE. The jungle is dense, confusing, full of hidden dangers — just like a tough reading passage. But a trained explorer knows how to navigate. They look for paths, read the signs, and find their way through. Today, we learn to navigate the jungle of text. Grab your survival kits — let’s move out!”

Hand out: Explorer Journals, Survival Badge Cards, Survival Kits (pencil pouches)

Survival Training 1 (45 min)

Reading/ELA Focus: 1. (10 min) “Trail Markers” Warm-Up — 5 quick questions on whiteboards. “Trail markers help you find your way. Key words in questions are YOUR trail markers.” 2. (20 min) “Machete Through the Text” — Teacher models close reading/annotation of a dense STAAR passage. “We’re cutting through the jungle. Every time I find an important detail, I mark the trail.” Demonstrate highlighting, margin notes, underlining key evidence. 3. (15 min) “Buddy System” — Partners read a passage together. One reads aloud (slowly, like hiking through brush), the other marks trail markers (key details). Discuss and answer 3-4 questions together.

Rest Stop (5 min)

  • “Animal Sounds” brain break — teacher calls an animal, students make the sound and a movement (monkey = ooh-ooh + arm swings, snake = hisssss + slither). Silly. Effective.

Survival Training 2 (40 min)

“Jungle Survival Challenge” Packet - STAAR-format reading passages with questions - Themed: “You found this message carved into a tree. Read it carefully…” - Early finishers: “Hidden Treasure” bonus challenge

Differentiation: - Struggling: “Trail Guide” version — shorter passages, vocabulary pre-taught, guided annotation marks provided - Advanced: “Uncharted Territory” — complex texts with analytical questions - ELL: Visual vocabulary cards, bilingual key terms

Tribal Council (10 min)

  • Quick review game: Teacher reads a question, students vote (thumbs up/down for answer choices A-D). Discuss the correct “verdict.”
  • Award Jungle Survival Badges 🌴
  • Update Journey Map: Jungle = CONQUERED

🏜️ TUESDAY — Desert Terrain

Theme: “The desert is harsh and unforgiving. Resources are scarce. Every drop of water counts — just like every step in a math problem counts.”

Focus: Mathematics

Trail Briefing (10 min)

Teacher Script: > “Explorers, we survived the jungle! Today we enter the DESERT. In the desert, you can’t waste anything. Every resource matters. Math is like desert survival — every step in your work matters. Skip a step, and you’re lost. Show ALL your work today. Conserve your energy. Be precise. The desert rewards the careful and punishes the careless. Let’s cross this desert!”

Survival Training 1 (45 min)

Math Focus: 1. (10 min) “Water Rationing” Warm-Up — 5 computation problems. “Each correct answer = one water bottle for your team. Get them wrong, and you’re dehydrated!” 2. (20 min) “Map & Compass” Guided Practice — Teacher models multi-step word problems as navigation challenges. “We need to calculate the distance to the next oasis…” Show every step. Emphasize: “In the desert, skipping steps = getting lost.” 3. (15 min) “Resource Sharing” — Partners work through 6 problems. Each partner solves 3 independently, then they check each other’s work. “Your buddy’s life depends on your accuracy!”

Rest Stop (5 min)

  • “Mirage or Real?” — Teacher shows 5 quick math statements. Students decide: true (real) or false (mirage). Stand up for real, sit for mirage.

Survival Training 2 (40 min)

“Desert Survival Challenge” Packet - STAAR-format math problems - Themed intro: “You need to calculate your way across the desert. One wrong answer and you veer off course.”

Differentiation: - Struggling: Reference sheet, worked examples, fewer problems - Advanced: “Scorching Challenge” — complex multi-step problems - ELL: Simplified word problem language, math vocabulary visuals

Tribal Council (10 min)

  • “Oasis or Mirage?” — Teacher presents 3 worked-out problems. Some have errors hidden in them. Students vote on whether each is correct (oasis) or has an error (mirage). Find the errors together.
  • Award Desert Survival Badges 🏜️
  • Journey Map update: Desert = CONQUERED

⛰️ WEDNESDAY — Mountain Terrain

Theme: “Mountains are the ultimate challenge. You can see the summit, but the climb is steep. Science and social studies are our mountains to climb.”

Focus: Science & Social Studies (or continued ELA/Math review)

Trail Briefing (10 min)

Teacher Script: > “We’ve cleared the jungle and crossed the desert. Now we face the MOUNTAINS. The climb is steep, but the view from the top? Incredible. Today we tackle science and social studies — the peaks of knowledge. Every fact you learn, every concept you master, gets you one step closer to the summit. And remember — we climb together. No explorer left behind!”

Survival Training 1 (45 min)

Science Review: 1. (10 min) “Base Camp Check” — 5 rapid-fire science questions on whiteboards 2. (20 min) “Mountain Stations” — 4-5 stations around the room, each a different “elevation” (difficulty) and science topic. Students rotate in groups (5 min each). Each station has a terrain flavor: “Elevation 1000ft: Matter & Energy,” “Elevation 3000ft: Force & Motion,” etc. 3. (15 min) “Trail Journal” — Students write in Explorer Journals: draw a compass rose with 4 science topics on each point, add key facts along each direction.

Rest Stop (5 min)

  • “Mountain Goat Balance” — Students stand on one foot for 30 seconds. Switch. Silly balance challenge. “Mountain goats never fall!”

Survival Training 2 (40 min)

Social Studies + Mixed Review: 1. (15 min) “History Hike” Timeline — Collaborative timeline activity (same as STAAR Wars but themed as a mountain trail — events placed at increasing “elevations”) 2. (25 min) “Mountain Challenge” Packet — Mixed science/social studies STAAR questions

Tribal Council (10 min)

  • Full Tribal Council Game: Teams of 4-5. Teacher reads a question with 4 answer choices. Each team discusses (1 min), then “votes” by holding up A/B/C/D cards. Teams that voted correctly survive. Teams that voted wrong are “on the chopping block” — they must answer a bonus question to stay in the game. Last team standing wins.
  • Award Mountain Survival Badges ⛰️
  • Journey Map: Mountains = CONQUERED
  • Midweek treat! 🎉

🌊 THURSDAY — Ocean Terrain

Theme: “The ocean is vast and deep. Today we take a deep dive — full STAAR review, navigating the currents of a mixed test.”

Focus: Mixed STAAR-format practice (all subjects)

Trail Briefing (10 min)

Teacher Script: > “Explorers. Jungle, desert, mountains — all conquered. Now we face the OCEAN. The ocean is unpredictable. Currents can pull you off course. Waves can knock you down. But if you stay calm and use your skills, you’ll swim right through. Today is our deep dive — a full practice crossing all subjects. Treat it like the real STAAR. Stay focused. Breathe. Navigate the currents.”

Survival Training 1 (45 min)

“Deep Dive Simulation” - Mini STAAR-format practice test (20-30 mixed questions) - Test-like conditions: silent, independent, timed - “You’re swimming across the ocean. Every correct answer is a stroke closer to shore.”

Rest Stop (5 min)

  • Deep breathing exercise: “When you’re swimming and you get tired, you float. Close your eyes. Breathe. Float.”

Survival Training 2 (40 min)

“Surfacing” Analysis & Review 1. (15 min) Review answers together 2. (10 min) Students fill out self-assessment: strengths, weak areas, strategy adjustments 3. (15 min) Small group “rescue teams” — students grouped by need, targeted review

Tribal Council (10 min)

  • Quick Tribal Council vote on 3 tough questions from the simulation
  • Award Ocean Survival Badges 🌊
  • Journey Map: Ocean = CONQUERED
  • “Tomorrow we reach the SUMMIT. One more terrain. You’ve got this.”

🏔️ FRIDAY — The Summit

Theme: “You’ve crossed every terrain. Today you reach the summit. Look how far you’ve come. You’re ready.”

Focus: Test strategies, confidence, celebration

Trail Briefing (10 min)

Teacher Script: > “Explorers. Look at your journey map. Jungle — conquered. Desert — conquered. Mountains — conquered. Ocean — conquered. Today, you reach the SUMMIT. The summit isn’t about learning new things. It’s about standing at the top and seeing everything clearly. Today we review our strategies, celebrate our journey, and prepare for the STAAR. You are SURVIVORS. You are ready.”

Survival Training 1 (45 min)

  1. (15 min) “Survival Guide Review” — Review top 10 test-taking strategies framed as survival rules: “Rule #1: Never eat unknown berries = Never pick an answer you can’t prove with evidence.”
  2. (15 min) “Campfire Stories” — Teacher reads test-taking strategies as adventure tales. “Once, an explorer was lost in a reading passage. She couldn’t find the main idea. But then she remembered: look at the first and last paragraph. The trail markers were there all along…”
  3. (15 min) “Final Challenge” Fun Review Game — Quick, light-stakes game. Celebrate right answers loudly.

Rest Stop (5 min)

  • Quiet meditation: “Close your eyes. You’re standing on the summit. Feel the wind. You made it.”

Survival Training 2 (40 min)

  1. (10 min) “Letters from the Summit” — Students write a letter to themselves from the top of the mountain
  2. (10 min) “Survival Certificate” Ceremony — Award certificates to each explorer. Make it meaningful.
  3. (10 min) “Summit Photo” — Class photo, students sign each other’s Explorer Journals
  4. (10 min) Celebration — Treats, music, joy. “You survived STAAR-vival!”

Final Tribal Council

  • Award Summit Survival Badges 🏔️
  • All 5 badges = “Ultimate Survivor” status
  • Journey Map: ALL TERRAINS CONQUERED 🎉
  • Final class cheer: “We SURVIVED! We’re STAAR READY!”

🌿 STAAR-vival — Themed Activities

12 survival-themed activities. Includes the legendary Tribal Council game.

🎮 REVIEW GAMES

1. Tribal Council (End-of-Day Review Game)

Type: Whole class teams | Time: 10-15 min | Best for: Daily review (any subject)

Setup: - Divide class into 3-4 “tribes” (table groups) - Teacher is the “Council Leader”

How to Play: 1. Teacher reads a STAAR-format question aloud 2. Tribes huddle and discuss (60 seconds) 3. Each tribe holds up their answer (A/B/C/D card) 4. Correct tribes “survive.” Wrong tribes get a “strike” 5. 3 strikes = tribe is “voted off the island” (they still play but can’t win the final prize) 6. Last tribe standing wins daily Tribal Council

Variations: - Immunity Challenge: One question where tribes can wager their strikes — get it right, remove a strike. Get it wrong, add one. - Hidden Immunity Idol: One random question is a “gimme” — all tribes that attempt it get a strike removed - Merge: On Friday, dissolve tribes. It’s every explorer for themselves.

Materials: A/B/C/D cards (one set per tribe), questions, scoreboard for strikes

2. Wilderness Escape Room

Type: Small groups (3-4) | Time: 30-40 min | Best for: Mixed subject review

Setup: - 5 “terrain locks” (envelopes), each with 4-5 questions - Correct answers generate a code - Final code unlocks the “escape from the wilderness” (prize envelope)

How to Play: 1. Groups work through Lock 1 (Jungle) → Lock 2 (Desert) → Lock 3 (Mountain) → Lock 4 (Ocean) → Lock 5 (Summit) 2. Must solve all questions correctly to get each code 3. Bring code to teacher to verify before moving to next lock 4. First group to crack all 5 and “escape” wins 5. All groups that finish earn “Escape Expert” badges

Materials: 5 envelopes per group, question sets, code answer key, prize envelope

3. Predator vs. Prey (Speed Review)

Type: Class game | Time: 15 min | Best for: Vocabulary, facts, computation

Setup: - Two students stand (Predator and Prey) - Teacher asks a question - First to answer correctly “survives” and stays standing - Loser sits, next person stands as the new challenger

How to Play: 1. Predator and Prey face each other 2. Teacher asks a question 3. First correct answer = that student is the Predator (stays) 4. Loser becomes Prey and sits, next person in line stands 5. Whoever has the longest “survival streak” wins

Materials: Question list, optional bell/buzzer

👤 INDIVIDUAL ACTIVITIES

4. Explorer Journal (Daily Reflection)

Type: Individual | Time: 10-15 min | Best for: Writing practice, metacognition

Instructions: Daily journal entries in their Explorer Journal (a booklet or stapled pages): - Monday (Jungle): “Today I navigated through… The thickest part was… I found my way by…” - Tuesday (Desert): “Resources are scarce. The math skill I need to conserve/strengthen is… My plan…” - Wednesday (Mountains): “The steepest climb was… I conquered it by…” - Thursday (Ocean): “The current pulled me toward… I stayed on course by…” - Friday (Summit): “From the summit I can see everything I’ve learned… My advice to future explorers…”

Materials: Explorer Journal booklet (5 pages + cover), pencils

5. Survival Guide Creation (Study Tool)

Type: Individual | Time: 25 min | Best for: End-of-topic synthesis

Instructions: Students create a pocket-sized “Survival Guide” — a folded booklet with essential knowledge organized by terrain: - Jungle Section (Reading): Top 3 reading strategies - Desert Section (Math): Key formulas and steps - Mountain Section (Science/SS): Must-know facts - Ocean Section (Mixed): Common mistakes to avoid - Summit Section (Strategies): Test-day game plan

Fold a single sheet of paper into an 8-page mini-book (search “one-page book fold” for instructions).

Materials: 1 sheet of paper per student, scissors, pencils/markers

6. Terrain Mapping (Concept Web)

Type: Individual | Time: 15-20 min | Best for: Organizing knowledge visually

Instructions: Students draw a map with 5 terrain zones. In each zone, they place key concepts for that day’s subject: - Draw terrain features (trees for jungle, cacti for desert, etc.) - Write concepts as “landmarks” within each terrain - Draw paths connecting related concepts across terrains - Add a “You Are Here” marker for their current confidence level

Materials: Large paper, colored pencils/markers

👥 PARTNER ACTIVITIES

7. Buddy System Navigation (Partner Problem Solving)

Type: Partner | Time: 20 min | Best for: Math, multi-step reading questions

Instructions: - Partners are “trail buddies” — they must stay together (agree on each answer before moving forward) - Work through a set of 8 problems - For each problem: discuss strategy FIRST (1 min), then solve together - Both partners must be able to explain the answer — teacher randomly checks - “In the wilderness, you never leave your buddy behind!”

Materials: Problem set, paper, pencils

8. SOS Signal (Partner Quiz Creation)

Type: Partner | Time: 20 min | Best for: Any subject review

Instructions: Each pair creates an “SOS Signal” — a set of 5 questions about their weakest topics (things they need to be “rescued” from). Then they swap with another pair and answer each other’s SOS signals. If they can answer another pair’s SOS, they’ve “rescued” them!

Materials: Paper, pencils

👥👥 GROUP ACTIVITIES

9. Base Camp Stations (Rotation)

Type: Groups of 4-5 | Time: 30-35 min | Best for: Multi-topic review

Setup: 5 stations, each themed as a terrain: - 🌴 Jungle Station — Reading comprehension questions - 🏜️ Desert Station — Math computation and word problems - ⛰️ Mountain Station — Science review - 🌊 Ocean Station — Social studies / mixed review - 🏔️ Summit Station — Test-taking strategy scenarios (“What would you do if…?”)

Groups rotate every 6 minutes. Record answers on an expedition log sheet.

Materials: Station question sets, expedition log sheets, timer, terrain labels

10. Campfire Stories (Read-Aloud Strategy Tales)

Type: Whole class or small groups | Time: 15-20 min | Best for: Test strategy review (Friday)

Instructions: Teacher (or student volunteers) reads test-taking strategies as short adventure tales:

Example: “Once, deep in the jungle of a reading passage, Explorer Maria was lost. The question asked for the ‘main idea,’ but every paragraph seemed to say something different. She was about to panic — but then she remembered her training. ‘Check the first sentence of each paragraph,’ her trail guide had taught her. She went back. She read each opening sentence. And there it was — a common thread running through every paragraph. The main idea emerged from the jungle like a clearing in the trees. Maria marked her answer with confidence and moved on.”

Write 5 of these (one per strategy). Students LOVE this format — it makes strategies memorable.

Materials: Pre-written strategy stories (teacher creates or adapts)

11. Survival Challenge Relay

Type: Teams of 5-6 | Time: 15-20 min | Best for: Quick review, computation, vocabulary

Instructions: Each team is a “survival crew.” Line up relay-style. 1. First explorer runs to the front, grabs a “supply” (question card), answers it 2. Correct = bring the supply back to base camp. Wrong = put it back, next person tries 3. Team must collect all 6 supplies (answer all 6 questions) to “survive” 4. First team to collect all supplies wins

Materials: Question cards in envelopes, answer key

12. Expedition Map (Collaborative Poster)

Type: Groups of 3-4 | Time: 25-30 min | Best for: Synthesis, study guide creation

Instructions: Each group creates a large illustrated “expedition map” for one subject/topic: - Draw a path from “Start” to “Summit” - Along the path, add key concepts as landmarks - Add “Danger Zones” (common mistakes) with warning signs - Add “Safe Havens” (helpful strategies) with shelter icons - Add “Treasure” (bonus tips) hidden along the route

Post all maps on the wall — they become collaborative study guides.

Materials: Butcher paper or poster board, markers, textbooks/notes

🌴 HALLWAY: Wilderness Trail

The Vision

The hallway becomes a trail through different terrains. Jungle vines hang from above, animal tracks on the floor lead to classrooms, and each door is a different “base camp.”

Step-by-Step

1. Jungle Vines

  • Twist green crepe paper streamers into vine shapes. Hang from ceiling/top of walls
  • Add paper leaves cut from green construction paper (various shades)
  • Twist brown paper bags into “tree branches” and tape to walls near doorframes
  • Cost: ~$4-6 for streamers + construction paper

2. Animal Tracks on the Floor

  • Cut animal footprints from brown/black construction paper (paw prints, bird tracks, boot prints)
  • Tape to the floor creating a trail from the hallway entrance to each classroom
  • Or use painter’s tape to make simple track shapes
  • Cost: ~$2-3 for paper, $2 for tape

3. Base Camp Door Signs

  • Each classroom door = a different base camp
  • “Base Camp [Teacher Name] — [Terrain]: [Subject]”
  • Example: “Base Camp Rodriguez — Desert Terrain: Mathematics”
  • Print on brown cardstock (looks like parchment/wood)
  • Add terrain-appropriate graphics (cacti, trees, waves, mountains)
